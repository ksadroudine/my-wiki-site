## Summary

AI agents now research and compare products for consumers, so brands are judged on observable performance such as price and availability and not on messaging alone, which makes the chief marketing officer's role more consequential. BCG's 2026 survey of about 300 chief marketing officers finds that nearly all say AI is transforming marketing, yet many still use generative AI only as a task assistant and few have agents that run entire workflows. The research describes a four-layer marketing stack and the need to align the brand promise with real performance. It also covers the demand for far more content at far lower cost in consumer goods, and the finding that agents become reliable only after expert practice has been written down and encoded for them.

## Highlights

- In BCG's 2026 survey of about 300 CMOs, 96% say AI is driving end-to-end transformation of their function, yet 42% still use generative AI only as a task assistant; maturity tiers are Leaders (32%), Followers (26%) and At-Risk (42%), and only about 8% connect multiple agents to run campaigns autonomously. <span class="src">Moving the Agentic Marketing Transformation from Illusion to Reality</span>
- Marketing leads its own AI investment (about half of CMOs say marketing decides, versus 14% the CEO or board), 43% of CMOs spent more than $15 million on marketing AI this year (28% last year), and 94% report sharply higher CEO expectations; BCG client results include 20-30% cost efficiency, threefold marketing ROI and tenfold faster campaign cycles. <span class="src">Moving the Agentic Marketing Transformation from Illusion to Reality</span>
- Discoverability is the new battleground: 90% of CMOs agree GenAI is reshaping how consumers find and judge brands, a majority are setting up agentic and generative engine optimisation teams, and 91% of B2C CMOs say AI-moderated, no-click discovery is reshaping their funnels. <span class="src">Moving the Agentic Marketing Transformation from Illusion to Reality</span>
- The agentic marketing stack has four layers (data, a brand-intelligence layer that encodes brand rules and trusted sources, an agentic orchestration layer, and a unified marketer interface), and martech and data are now the top investment area, up 11-12 points since 2025. <span class="src">Moving the Agentic Marketing Transformation from Illusion to Reality</span>
- AI agents evaluate brands on observable performance, not messaging, and nearly half of responses from major AI platforms contain unsolicited recommendations, so brand promise must be aligned with performance (pricing, availability, fulfilment, service) across the enterprise. <span class="src">Agentic AI Will Make the CMO’s Role More Consequential</span>
- Agent conversations reveal customer jobs-to-be-done and co-mingled cross-category demand, 43% of consumer journeys are research-led, touchpoints have risen from about 5 to over 15 in a decade, and 85% of consumers using AI in research rank it among their top five influences. <span class="src">Agentic AI Will Make the CMO’s Role More Consequential</span>
- A branded customer-facing agent is attractive for owning data and experience but may be trusted less than neutral comparison agents, and is most credible where privacy and long-term relationships matter, such as banking. <span class="src">Agentic AI Will Make the CMO’s Role More Consequential</span>
- CPG content needs have grown from roughly 500 assets a year to five or six million, while a personalised beauty product shot fell from $4 to four cents; insurgent brands like David Protein and Poppi are taking share, and small brands gained 10.8 points in cereal and granola bars while incumbents lost over 12. <span class="src">Why Most CEOs Are Failing Marketing’s New Math Test</span>
- Insights agents only become reliable when expert practice is codified: a global CPG firm's campaign-brief agent rose from 50% to over 90% accuracy after encoding how top marketers worked, saving 3,000-plus marketers weeks of effort each year. <span class="src">Why Most CEOs Are Failing Marketing’s New Math Test</span>

## Concept

Three BCG pieces from mid-2026 describe the same shift from different seats. <span class="src">Moving the Agentic Marketing Transformation from Illusion to Reality</span> reports the CMO survey and diagnoses an "illusion" gap: almost all CMOs say transformation is under way, but maturity grouped by actual deployment (workflows touched, orchestration of agents, effect on productivity and ROI) shows 42% at risk of an illusory transformation, mostly piloted tools with productivity gains that have not scaled and no change to operating model or martech stack. Spending is moving in waves — first disconnected tools, then connective tissue (data, measurement, digital experience, talent), now the operating infrastructure — and leading CMOs ask "what operating system will let my tools, agents and workflows function together?" rather than "which tool should we buy?" They build new roles (AI product owners, AI governance owners, engine-optimisation teams), reorganise around objectives or customer segments instead of channels, and treat upskilling as the critical enabler (about 80% invest in AI-specific training, with AI academies, hackathons, super-user groups and "talent doesn't exist, I have to create it" as a recurring refrain). Risks have shifted from fears about creativity and authenticity to security, privacy, legal exposure and brand guardrails. <span class="src">Moving the Agentic Marketing Transformation from Illusion to Reality</span>

<span class="src">Agentic AI Will Make the CMO’s Role More Consequential</span> explains why the stakes are higher: customers increasingly use AI to discover, evaluate and buy, so marketers must now market to two decision makers, humans and the AI systems that influence them. It argues that only 14% of CEOs and CFOs consider their CMO highly effective at driving growth (per Gartner), and that the opportunity is to rebuild three pillars together. Brand stewardship shifts from narrative to promise-performance alignment, since agents compare complaint patterns, availability and pricing integrity and expose gaps between what a brand says and delivers (a financial firm claiming trust but showing high claim-rejection rates), so CMOs should assess their brand the way agents will, integrate operational signals into marketing decisions and set cross-enterprise governance. Market intelligence shifts from keywords to expressed intent, as prompts reveal motivations and cross-category needs (Google's AI Mode queries are triple the length of ordinary searches, and "which one" language is the fastest-growing type), letting marketing feed R&D, bundling and partnerships. Customer experience must be designed for parallel human and agent influence pathways, measured through share of mentions, citations and recommendation rates, with a deliberate decision on whether to deploy a branded agent. The marketing operating model should move from channel structures to intelligence-led models that direct scarce people and budget toward growth. <span class="src">Agentic AI Will Make the CMO’s Role More Consequential</span>

<span class="src">Why Most CEOs Are Failing Marketing’s New Math Test</span> addresses the CEO, arguing that consumer-goods leaders still read marketing budgets through the mass-media era's assumption that scale guarantees share. Four dynamics rewrite the equation: content volume explodes while unit costs collapse; humans, agents and agencies divide work differently (strategists orchestrate end to end, agents execute, agencies move from percentage-of-spend to value-sharing compensation); returns shift as content costs fall while application, cloud and token spend rise; and AI changes both what marketers can know and how consumers decide (nearly a third of shoppers use AI in the purchase journey, a threefold increase in 18 months, and 13% buy what it recommends). Its five CEO questions ask whether AI is being used to spend less or grow more, whether marketing impact is measured in a trusted way (a brand-health scorecard embedded within six months, with measurement kept in-house), how end-to-end processes are being reshaped, how unique, non-replicable advantage is being built from proprietary knowledge without vendor lock-in, and whether investment targets the emerging customer journey or the known one. <span class="src">Why Most CEOs Are Failing Marketing’s New Math Test</span>

## Open Questions

- The 20% to 30% efficiency and threefold ROI results are BCG client engagements reported without baselines or sample size, and the survey's 31% (B2C) and 20% (B2B) significant-revenue-impact figures are self-reported by CMOs.
- It is unresolved whether a branded agent helps or hurts a brand against neutral comparison agents; BCG identifies conditions (trust, privacy, relationship depth) but offers no evidence of outcomes.
- The sources do not reconcile BCG's call to retain measurement and proprietary intelligence in-house with its simultaneous advice to rely on partners and agencies for execution.
