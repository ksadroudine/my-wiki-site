## Summary

McKinsey's Global Technology and Telecommunications B2B Survey, run across three consecutive editions from 2025 through 2026, tracks how B2B customer expectations of telecom operators keep shifting toward integrated solutions beyond core connectivity. Security, omnichannel engagement, and — most recently — agentic AI emerge as the recurring drivers of growth, loyalty, and provider-switching behavior, independently corroborated by Accenture's separate 1,200-leader survey.

## Highlights

- Across three consecutive survey years, roughly 80% of B2B customers consistently affirm that telecom operators have a "right to play" beyond core connectivity, yet most operators have not capitalized on that mandate.
- Core connectivity has become table stakes with slowing growth (projected under 5% annually by 2026), while beyond-core domains — security, cloud, agentic AI — grow two to three times faster or more, reshaping where telco revenue opportunity actually sits.
- McKinsey's four-play framework lays out the strategic options for beyond-core growth: the connectivity play (refocusing the core portfolio around near-core products like SD-WAN), the ICT play (bundling cybersecurity and cloud services), the 5G network API play (monetizing network capabilities), and the ecosystem-building play (standalone businesses in verticals like healthcare or automotive) — together worth an estimated $160 billion in additional revenue by 2028.
- Security is the most consistent driver across all three survey years, repeatedly ranking as the top reason B2B customers switch telecom providers, ahead of price, coverage, and service reliability.
- Omnichannel engagement moved from a nice-to-have to a baseline expectation across the three surveys: customers increasingly want a seamless blend of digital, remote, and human-touchpoint interactions across the entire buying journey, including for high-value transactions.
- The most recent survey wave identifies agentic AI as the newest and most powerful accelerant of digital-channel growth: enterprises with agentic AI fully implemented at scale report willingness to transact up to a third more value through fully digital, end-to-end channels than peers using AI more selectively.
- Data privacy, sovereignty, and regulatory compliance have become a "gating factor rather than a differentiator" in the most recent survey, as agentic AI systems gain broader access to enterprise data — nearly two-thirds of B2B customers now require private cloud or on-premise deployment.
- Despite strong demand and customer openness (98% open to AI-assisted service in at least one interaction), at-scale agentic AI deployment remains rare — only 3 to 11% of surveyed customers report full-scale deployment for any given use case, even in the most mature use case, customer care.
- Customer stickiness toward telecom operators is stabilizing overall but is becoming increasingly conditional on trust and security rather than connectivity alone; cloud services and communications/collaboration remain structurally contested domains where incumbency offers limited protection.
- No provider type — foundation-model/AI-native vendors, system integrators, or telecom operators themselves — has yet established a clearly dominant position in delivering agentic AI solutions to B2B customers, leaving the opportunity for operators open but unproven.
- Independent research from Accenture (1,200+ enterprise leaders surveyed) corroborates McKinsey's "right to play" finding with different numbers pointing the same direction: 73% of enterprises rank telcos among their top three providers for integrated B2B solutions, over 70% prefer telco partnerships specifically for local and connected AI data centers, and 60% express interest in working with telcos on AI agents and industry-tailored AI-as-a-Service offerings. <span class="src">Reinventing B2B for the AI economy</span>
- Despite that preference, nearly half of enterprises report dissatisfaction with telcos' actual connectivity and digital/IT delivery — self-service network provisioning, standardized configuration APIs, and total-cost-of-ownership improvements are the specific gaps cited — meaning telcos hold latent demand they are currently failing to convert, echoing this concept's other sources' warning that preference alone hasn't yet translated into deployed capability. <span class="src">Reinventing B2B for the AI economy</span>
- Accenture's three-part repositioning framework for capturing this opportunity: build a connected infrastructure platform (an integrated connectivity-cloud-edge-AI continuum, not connectivity alone); elevate to an outcome-based, partner-driven business model rather than pure direct sales; and redesign the operating and talent model to match the new portfolio and go-to-market motion. <span class="src">Reinventing B2B for the AI economy</span>

## Concept

<span class="src">Playing to win in B2B telecom</span> (McKinsey, February 2025) opens the series by establishing the strategic frame that the later editions build on. Drawing on McKinsey's B2B Telco & Tech Pulse Survey, it finds that 80 percent of decision-makers see telecom operators as viable partners beyond core connectivity, with beyond-core spending expected to grow two to three times faster than core spending. The article distills operators' growth options into four distinct strategies: the connectivity play (refocusing the core portfolio around near-core products such as SD-WAN), the ICT play (delivering cybersecurity and cloud services alongside connectivity), the 5G network API play (monetizing network capabilities through APIs for use cases like fraud detection or dynamic video-quality adjustment), and the ecosystem-building play (creating standalone businesses in verticals such as healthcare or automotive, exemplified by Telstra Health and Vodafone Automotive). Combined, the four plays represent an estimated $160 billion in additional revenue potential by 2028, each with distinct financial return profiles, capability requirements, and degrees of departure from a telco's traditional business.

<span class="src">B2B customers have spoken. Will telcos listen?</span> (McKinsey, June 2025) follows four months later with fresh survey data reinforcing and sharpening the same beyond-core thesis. It confirms the same "right to play" finding (nearly 80 percent) and adds granularity: cybersecurity is the single most compelling B2B priority across all regions and company sizes, and it has become the leading cited reason customers switch telecom providers, ahead of brand, product capability, and price. The article documents a structural shift toward digital B2B buying, noting that fully digital channels are expected to nearly double in preference across buying stages within 12 months, and that the volume of high-value transactions (over €1 million) conducted through end-to-end digital self-service has tripled since 2022. It closes with a warning that roughly one in four B2B customers plans to switch telco vendors within the next year, primarily toward cloud providers and tech manufacturers, making the shift in customer intent — even before it fully materializes in switching behavior — a signal operators cannot ignore.

<span class="src">Winning B2B customers in technology and telecommunications</span> (McKinsey, February 2026) is the most recent installment and introduces agentic AI as the central new variable reshaping the same underlying dynamics the first two editions established. It confirms that the fundamentals identified a year earlier are holding — strong "right to play" recognition, omnichannel as baseline expectation, security as the leading switching trigger — while adding that security and agentic AI are now the fastest-growing spending domains, each expanding more than 9 percent annually, as core connectivity growth slows below 5 percent. The article's central finding is that agentic AI maturity is now directly correlated with B2B customers' willingness to transact digitally: enterprises with agentic AI fully deployed at scale report average digital "ticket sizes" roughly twice those of peers still in early pilots, and each step up the AI-maturity curve is associated with a 20 to 45 percent increase in the dollar value customers are willing to transact end-to-end online. It also identifies data privacy and sovereignty as an emerging non-negotiable buying criterion — more than half of respondents cite it as their top concern — and finds that despite near-universal customer openness to AI-assisted service (98 percent), actual full-scale agentic AI deployment remains rare (3 to 11 percent by use case), leaving the provider landscape for agentic AI solutions still wide open, with telecom operators, system integrators, and AI-native vendors all in contention and none dominant.

<span class="src">Reinventing B2B for the AI economy</span> (Accenture, July 2025) supplies independent corroboration of the McKinsey series' core finding, drawn from Accenture's own survey of more than 1,200 enterprise leaders rather than McKinsey's panel. The numbers point the same direction from a different angle: 73 percent of enterprises rank telcos among their top three providers of choice for integrated B2B solutions, over 70 percent specifically prefer telco partnerships for local and connected AI data centers (citing telcos' data proximity, security posture, and existing network infrastructure as the differentiators), and 60 percent express interest in working with telcos on AI agents and industry-tailored AI-as-a-Service offerings — figures that echo, from an entirely separate research program, the "right to play" mandate the McKinsey series documents. But Accenture's research also sharpens the gap between preference and delivery this concept's other sources leave more implicit: nearly half of surveyed enterprises report active dissatisfaction with telcos' current connectivity and digital/IT execution, citing specific unmet expectations around self-service network provisioning, standardized configuration APIs, and total-cost-of-ownership improvements. Accenture's prescription for closing that gap is a three-part repositioning: building a connected infrastructure platform that integrates connectivity, cloud, edge, and AI into one continuum rather than treating them as separate product lines; elevating to an outcome-based, partner-driven business model that balances direct sales with ecosystem engagement, since modern enterprise buyers increasingly expect to pay for results rather than raw capacity; and redesigning the underlying operating and talent model so organizational structure, technology stack, and hiring actually match the new portfolio rather than lagging behind it. <span class="src">Reinventing B2B for the AI economy</span>

Read together, the four sources — three waves of the same McKinsey survey program plus one independent Accenture study — trace a consistent and deepening narrative: B2B telecom customers have steadily expected more than connectivity from their operators since at least early 2025, security has remained the single most durable driver of both spending growth and switching behavior across every edition, and the newest wave shows agentic AI compounding that dynamic by simultaneously raising customer expectations, increasing digital transaction sizes, and elevating data governance to a gating requirement rather than a differentiator — while the independently-sourced Accenture research confirms these aren't McKinsey-specific findings and adds the sharpest available evidence that operators' actual delivery is currently the binding constraint, not customer demand.

## Open Questions

- None of the three survey editions report a consistent year-over-year respondent panel, so it is not established whether the shifts described (e.g., rising SME confidence, stabilizing stickiness) reflect the same enterprises changing behavior or a changing survey sample.
- The 2026 edition's finding that operators rank among B2B customers' top three preferred agentic AI providers is explicitly flagged by McKinsey as aspirational rather than reflective of current market reality; no source clarifies what specific capability gap is preventing operators from converting that latent preference into actual deployments.
