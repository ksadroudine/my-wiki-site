## Summary

Forrester's research treats marketing technology as a strategic asset and not a tactical purchase. It argues that chief marketing officers should own it personally and build it as a flexible ecosystem of connected parts, centered on interoperability, data, orchestration and generative AI, instead of buying a single vendor's suite. The frameworks cover the capabilities a stack needs and an eight-stage cycle that keeps investment tied to business goals. They also cover a path for moving generative AI from hype and pilot projects into production, using a map of AI capabilities, groups of companies by maturity and a method that covers people, process, implementation and measurement. A parallel maturity assessment for business-to-business go-to-market strategy shows how to align sales, marketing and product around decisions that start from the audience, so that the technology serves a coherent market and buyer strategy.

## Concept

Forrester's earliest report in the research program, from 2021, argues that treating martech as a tactical solution undersells its impact, since few technology categories influence revenue, customer engagement and brand so directly. It names four dimensions of a strategic mission. Martech must engage customers by orchestrating relevant experiences across the full journey and not only isolated touchpoints, it must help marketers rather than constrain their creativity or understanding of customers through usable interfaces, fewer silos and visible insight into what works, it must build differentiated intellectual property that competitors cannot replicate, such as strong identity resolution that enables second-party data sharing in co-op marketing, and it must prepare marketers for continuous change without wholesale replatforming. <span class="src">The Moments-Based Evolution Of Enterprise Marketing Technology</span>

Even in 2021 the stack was under strain, since 91% of enterprise martech users cited integration as the most important feature of a marketing tool but only 19% of Enterprise Marketing Suite customer references believed one vendor could deliver every capability, which left marketers between suite and best-of-breed purchasing. The report recommends organizing priorities around value creation rather than feature accumulation, by eliminating any digital divide in the stack, since disconnected components actively harm, by reducing emphasis on commodity functions such as sending email, buying display ads and building dashboards in favor of capabilities that move core KPIs, and by taking ownership of strategically important systems, customer analytics as a system of insight and cross-channel campaign management as a system of engagement, because they generate the proprietary knowledge a customer-obsessed organization must control. <span class="src">The Moments-Based Evolution Of Enterprise Marketing Technology</span>

The six interdependent capabilities for next-generation martech are a holistic customer data management strategy that makes accessible, consistent data the foundation, identity resolution that assembles a unified picture of a customer across touchpoints, customer experience optimization that improves entire journeys instead of campaigns or channels one by one, contextually relevant content infrastructure that can create, manage, locate and repurpose creative for specific moments across any device, customer journey analytics that move measurement beyond clicks toward journey-level KPIs such as satisfaction and lifetime value, and unified orchestration that architects the journey in one coordinated workflow. Near-term steps do not need to wait for vendors, namely inventorying the current stack, which 74% of surveyed users had already tied to a department-wide strategy, weighting buying criteria toward integration over standalone function, as a regional retail bank did by replatforming three tools at once to validate integration across them, documenting an identity graph of which identifiers are used for which interactions and benchmarking campaign and business KPIs to support continuous monitoring. <span class="src">The Moments-Based Evolution Of Enterprise Marketing Technology</span>

The 2022 CMO's guide makes the case personal. There is no marketing without martech, which turns strategy into insight-driven live engagement, yet most companies still plan and deliver it in a decentralized, ad hoc way, buying tools one at a time instead of building a deliberately architected system. CMOs have the largest organizational stake and should take hands-on ownership of martech vision and management instead of delegating it to IT, and one mobile operator's CMO describes the commitment as about six months of four-to-eight-hour weekly self-education. Beyond commoditized data management, insight generation and campaign execution, a full stack must deliver five business capabilities, engagement velocity, the speed and volume needed for real-time experience, personalization, through tailored content, cross-channel reach and algorithms that choose channel, timing and offer, cohesive customer journeys, through unified profiles visible to both insight and engagement systems, agility, through a swappable, upgradable architecture using public cloud, low-code and headless technology, and protection, with native or integrated data security, preference, consent and privacy management. <span class="src">A CMO’s Guide to Marketing Technology</span>

Four architectural pillars organize these capabilities. The data fabric collects, manages and assembles internal and external data into unified customer profiles, customer understanding is the analysis layer of reporting, analytics, targeting and testing, brand strategy covers planning, resource, content and performance management that connect strategy to execution, and brand experience is the engagement layer that orchestrates lifecycle interactions across marketing, advertising and loyalty touchpoints. Years of tactical, reactive decisions have left most stacks fragmented, so building a complete, cohesive one is a colossal undertaking few fully achieve, though a source of advantage for those that do. Forrester finds three differentiators plus a fourth, staffing. An enterprise martech strategy is a unified plan that also funds future innovation, integration is both the top vendor-selection criterion and martech's biggest technical shortcoming, covering unified profiles, cross-application workflows and cross-touchpoint orchestration, and data management and identity resolution rest on a documented inventory of current and planned data needs backed by real collection, hygiene and resolution processes, as a digital-bank CMO who wanted a real-time view of the data without ceding control to an agency put it. The fourth is a dedicated marketing technologist to own strategy, roadmap and vendor relationships, since one clothing-brand CMO called hiring a director of martech breaking all standards at a company used to centralized IT and concluded that a part-time person cannot do the role. <span class="src">A CMO’s Guide to Marketing Technology</span>

The 2023 alignment framework supplies the process that keeps ownership effective, framing alignment as an evolving, iterative process rather than a one-time project, designed to hold up under four recurring sources of misalignment. Siloed decision-making lets choices optimized for one department create duplication for others who were not consulted, mergers and acquisitions force together infrastructures with different procurement histories and cultures, producing duplication, political conflict over process ownership and disrupted supplier relationships, emerging technologies arrive faster than governance can absorb, and new strategic initiatives require processes and technologies to change in ways that can stall the growth engine they were meant to speed. <span class="src">The Forrester B2C Marketing Technology Alignment Framework</span>

The framework runs in two phases across eight stages. In discovery and analysis, Initiate secures cross-organizational agreement on what alignment means in terms of shared business objectives, with a cross-functional charter and a common martech reference model, Audit baselines the current infrastructure by mapping tools to capabilities, processes and stakeholders and scoring functionality and adoption, repeated periodically, Analyze compares the baseline with the desired future state and decides whether gaps are best closed with people, process, technology or a combination, and Propose develops a business case for each project with estimated time, cost, benefit and risk. In execution and optimization, Prioritize evaluates proposals against guidelines and business objectives to select those with the greatest cross-organizational impact and secures authorization, Plan builds roadmaps with resources, financial projections, milestones and measurement criteria tied to the business case, Execute implements them while communicating purpose and benefit and highlighting interim wins in phased rollouts, and Govern reviews the infrastructure, sets responsibility and accountability, captures lessons, watches vendors' innovation roadmaps for new capability and uses Forrester's martech maturity model to decide where to focus. Govern feeds back into a renewed Initiate as business needs, vendor capabilities and market conditions shift. <span class="src">The Forrester B2C Marketing Technology Alignment Framework</span>

Two reports turn to generative AI, a year apart, to track the shift from hype to operations. The first, from December 2023, cautions against overhype because AI is not new to martech, since predictive AI had been standard for more than a decade, 54% of surveyed US B2C marketing executives already said AI contributed to marketing execution before genAI's public debut and only 15% had rated AI capability enhancement a top strategic priority as recently as mid-2022. Its tool is a two-dimensional map for any AI capability, with autonomy, collaborative AI in which the marketer retains final say versus independent AI that acts largely unsupervised, as lights-out automation already does in ad bidding and send-time optimization, crossed with technique, predictive AI, which identifies likely future outcomes from historical data and has been in use for over a decade, versus generative AI, which creates original content from natural-language prompts and is much newer. Beyond creative content it highlights emerging applications, dynamic insights that embed measurement in every interaction to optimize campaigns in real time and even generate synthetic data to simulate a journey's impact while in flight, collaborative workflows that extend analytical, creative and orchestration capability once reserved for specialists, high-velocity engagement that speeds the full marketing-operations cycle and anticipatory journeys that combine predictive and generative AI to generate journeys from prompts. <span class="src">Look Beyond The Hype To Evolve Martech With AI</span>

Because technical planning is often thin, with only half of surveyed AI decision-makers reporting a documented conceptual technical framework or architecture in their AI strategy, the report sets four prerequisites before adopting genAI. Sufficient data and training means working with IT, enterprise architecture and data science to gather and prepare company-specific data that tunes a public LLM toward on-brand output, since data quality management was already among the biggest execution challenges for 31% of B2C marketing decision-makers. Continuous monitoring means testing across the AI lifecycle, independent reviews for bias, hallucination and unintended consequences and an enterprise-wide governance framework, since 64% of AI decision-makers worried about misuse leading to organizational errors. Measurement to justify investment means tracking both marketing-KPI lift and efficiency gains so that time savings visibly fund further resources, training and skills, with 57% of AI decision-makers having documented outcomes, KPIs and ROI goals, and comprehensive privacy and security means privacy by design for the risk of putting sensitive business and customer data into public LLMs, amid regulatory action such as a US presidential executive order and EU restrictions on some open-source genAI and the third-party risk of accessing open-source models through martech vendors. <span class="src">Look Beyond The Hype To Evolve Martech With AI</span>

A February 2024 report shifts to maturity, starting from the observation that the genAI honeymoon is over, since more than a quarter of AI decision-makers cited integrating genAI with existing infrastructure as the biggest adoption barrier. It profiles four segments of US B2C marketing executives. Integrators, 26%, embed genAI teamwide in go-to-market execution, often through centralized agency platforms such as dentsu Connect or WPP Open, and increasingly focus on data quality as capabilities become multimodal, Explorers, 40%, pilot genAI in specific tasks and advance by adding use cases such as media operations or content localization while addressing employees' job-loss fears, Considerers, 29%, are interested but not yet experimenting and need to prioritize realistic use cases and assess technology, data and skills readiness, and Rejectors, 4%, do not intend to use genAI in execution, typically because of heavy regulation or unconvinced leadership, and can still benefit by outsourcing to external genAI marketing partners. Advancing matters because genAI is projected to enhance 4.5 times more marketing jobs than it eliminates, which frames adoption as an augmentation and skills challenge. <span class="src">Advance GenAI Marketing from Pilot Projects to Proficiency</span>

Its GenAI Marketing Adoption Framework has three steps. Establish adoption as an explicit objective by identifying outcomes tied to business goals, securing a CMO-level sponsor to legitimize pilots and unlock funding and building the investment case for AI provider spend. Invest in a task force to build expertise by finding passionate hand-raisers as evangelists, institutionalizing a repeatable test-and-learn method with 30-, 60- and 90-day measurement checkpoints and rewarding adaptability as pilots reveal what works. Create a playbook for responsible execution by institutionalizing continuous training beyond isolated pockets, building AI partner alliances for early access and promoting policies on tools and approved use cases calibrated to risk tolerance, and these mechanics of moving from Considerer to Explorer to Integrator are what the capability-mapping and production-scaling reports assume without detailing. <span class="src">Advance GenAI Marketing from Pilot Projects to Proficiency</span>

The March 2024 report broadens back to the structural evolution of the entire stack, arguing that legacy martech, built to set up and deliver outbound, largely email-centric campaigns, must evolve for the devices, channels and touchpoints where consumers now engage. Its next-generation requirements are customer-first data strategies, shifting toward first- and zero-party data, identity resolution and privacy-respecting profiles under ongoing data-deprecation pressure, the enduring personalization mandate, which 41% of B2C marketing decision-makers still cited among their biggest execution challenges and which no single technology solves, heightened expectations that martech investment demonstrably contributes to the business, with 66% planning to spend more in 2023 even as 26% cited economic conditions as a top challenge, and support for invisible experiences that anticipate customer needs across sales, service and digital touchpoints, which requires systems built for real-time scale. Legacy stacks fall short, since integration still topped feature priorities at 91% while only 19% believed a suite could cover their needs, automation and access remain inefficient, forcing analysts to rebuild data objects and move data across systems for each campaign, and few stacks were engineered for today's real-time decisioning volume, as an AARP data leader described making tens of millions of engagement decisions a day based on billions of pieces of information. <span class="src">The Future Of Marketing Technology</span>

Forrester's answer is to redefine martech as an ecosystem, an interconnected system of data, applications and services that enables consumer marketing strategy, insights and engagement, organized around customer understanding, brand strategy and brand experience but distinguished by integration and modularity over monolithic platforms, which lets marketers adapt to unresolved questions about data, privacy and consumer preference without replatforming, develop capability incrementally, reduce a single vendor's negotiating leverage and tune capabilities to company needs. Four strategic elements deserve investment. Interoperability covers integration, workflow and data connectivity so that modules work as one cohesive unit, data is the foundation requiring consolidated profiles, real-time streaming, enrichment connectivity and identity resolution, as a Google martech manager noted that whatever sits on top can be flexible but the data systems must be smart, orchestration is a unified facility for assembling audiences, communications and triggered interactions across touchpoints and lifecycle phases, and generative AI, already used by 26% of respondents with another 40% exploring, expands into automated insight, streamlined operations and generated journeys, as an AARP marketing leader called it a forcing factor for leadership's willingness to invest. <span class="src">The Future Of Marketing Technology</span>

Realizing the ecosystem requires organizational change. Consolidation should be de-emphasized as a goal, since no single vendor has delivered both technical efficiency and marketing efficacy and consolidation should be an outcome of good planning, strong partnerships should be valued across IT, enterprise architecture and data science, governance and planning should be dynamic for a more design-flexible ecosystem, data strategy should precede and outlast any technology choice, and the circle of collaborators should widen to include the CFO, for procurement, business cases and performance management, and CX counterparts for coordinating the technology and strategy of increasingly invisible journeys. <span class="src">The Future Of Marketing Technology</span>

The November 2024 report closes the gap between urgency and deployment, finding only 18% of surveyed B2B marketing decision-makers had advanced genAI from experimentation to production, with most still experimenting on historical data, 54%, within live production workflows, 53%, or in isolated sandboxes, 46%, and typically layering use cases on existing processes rather than building new capabilities. Common use cases are content generation, as a cloud-computing marketer generated verticalized copy for account-based campaigns, natural-language interfaces, as in ActionIQ's audience-segment creation, which returns both a plain-language explanation and the underlying query for validation, and application assistants, as in Adobe's assistant that answers questions such as how to create a campaign brief. To progress, the report proposes a four-dimension framework worked through for each use case rather than applied as a one-size rollout. On people, genAI adoption is a cross-functional project among marketers, accountable for investment, outcomes and the appropriateness of customer-facing output, IT, extending its management of the tech and data ecosystem to fast-changing LLMs, data scientists, who manage complexity, explainability and observability and validate performance, and stewards, who set governance and manage the data and model lifecycle across engineering, data science, security and legal. <span class="src">Shift Generative AI In Martech From Theory To Reality</span>

On process, a five-step iterative cycle starts with ideate, determining which use cases support customer objectives and marketing strategy by aligning top-down goals with bottom-up pain points, forecast, assessing ecosystem, team and cultural readiness and technical, ethical and legal risk, prototype, putting a working model in employees' hands, as GoDaddy's internal GoCaaS content-as-a-service platform lets every team experiment with predictive and generative models through a shared data layer, prioritize, weighing feasibility, effort and ROI, as Dell's chief AI officer used a three-criteria framework of customer experience, business growth and business productivity to narrow 800 use cases to 36, and activate, deciding whether to continue, pivot, pause or stop and communicating the decision with realistic scaling timelines, as an Adobe APAC customer started with image repurposing that saved USD 300 per image and used that proof to win buy-in from IT, legal and security. On implementation, three access paths have distinct trade-offs, genAI embedded in third-party technology is easiest and fastest but less customizable, may add costs and brings vendor-introduced privacy risk, public LLMs, open or closed, trade flexibility, control and data privacy that need technical expertise and budget for open models against easier integration and scale but higher access costs and less customization for closed ones, and building a proprietary model offers the most control but needs substantial engineering talent, compute, training data and upkeep. <span class="src">Shift Generative AI In Martech From Theory To Reality</span>

On measurement, a dimension few companies or vendors were discussing, genAI should be judged on efficiency, productivity gains and usage, journey and event analytics, as Persado cut customer content-revision requests by replacing manual brand-guideline checks with a brand-trained LLM review and approval process, and on effectiveness, incremental lift to existing measurement spanning CX metrics such as satisfaction and business metrics such as opens, clicks, conversions, attribution, revenue, acquisition cost and lifetime value, benchmarked by A/B testing against non-AI-assisted output, since in the words of one chief product officer generating a report or brief is great for productivity but CEOs should care about customer experience and revenue lift. <span class="src">Shift Generative AI In Martech From Theory To Reality</span>

Forrester's Q1 2024 landscape maps the vendor side of customer data capability. Customer analytics technologies turn data into customer-focused actions, buying has widened from the CMO to CX teams, digital product teams and IT, offerings have shifted from tools for scarce data scientists to do-it-for-me out-of-the-box models, a path Salesforce Einstein began in 2016, alongside custom modeling, and vendors are sized by category revenue, large at USD 250 million or more, medium at USD 100-250 million and small at USD 10-100 million. Legacy categories, CRM, customer data platforms, product analytics and digital intelligence, are converging as vendors chase buyers, siloed data producing a fractured customer view is the main challenge, generative AI, which lets users query data and build segments in natural language, is the main disruptor, core use cases are customer 360, retention and personalization, and Forrester describes an internal war for ownership of the customer, with a unified view as the prize, and expects vendors to move from insight to prescription, such as recommending a campaign to an automatically identified high-value, high-churn-risk segment. <span class="src">The Customer Analytics Technologies Landscape, Q1 2024</span>

The technology serves a go-to-market strategy, which Forrester's March 2024 maturity report treats as the root of many underperforming B2B efforts. Strategies have historically been built around internal constructs, product lines, business units and sales regions, instead of external constructs such as market segments and target audiences, a mismatch exposed as buyer behavior changes and digital channels multiply, and when executed well a strategy catalyzes growth by aligning sales, marketing and product around shared core decisions. The Go-To-Market Maturity Assessment scores four areas on a 1-to-5 agreement scale. Business context asks whether a well-defined, audience-first decision process exists with alignment among product, marketing and sales leaders, market strategy asks whether the most viable segments are identified and prioritized using both internal data and external research and not intuition, buyer strategy asks whether buying centers, buying groups and prioritized personas are documented well enough to fuel buyer-focused engagement and to show which offerings are best positioned, and engagement strategy asks whether activities are coordinated into a cohesive buyer experience through persona profiles, buyer's-journey maps and buyer messaging. <span class="src">Evaluate Your Go-To-Market Strategy Maturity</span>

Because go-to-market strategy is a corporate asset that touches every function, it usually falls to portfolio marketing to coordinate, which means navigating a product-first culture, the complexity of a multi-product portfolio and the tension between committing to a direction and staying opportunistic. At the beginner stage, functional teams run disparate strategies with little alignment and a product focus, so the priority is business context and market strategy first, since those decisions shape everything downstream, and finding an executive sponsor who believes in audience-centricity to champion the work across the C-suite, using Forrester's Go-To-Market Architecture as a starting guide. At the intermediate stage the basics are mastered, so the organization should find its lowest-scoring area, agree where to focus, for example using relative-targeting research to prioritize segments methodically, and tighten alignment across sales, product, marketing and customer success. At the advanced stage leadership is committed, most elements are mastered and internal and external insight drives execution, so the focus is incremental improvement such as refreshing or repackaging personas, collaborating with downstream colleagues and watching for triggers, such as leadership changes or shifts in market conditions or buyer behavior, that warrant revisiting the strategy. <span class="src">Evaluate Your Go-To-Market Strategy Maturity</span>
