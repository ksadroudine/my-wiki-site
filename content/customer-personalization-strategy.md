## Summary

Personalization is a compounding source of advantage, because its value grows with the number of customer interactions a company learns from and with the square of how fast it learns. The framework in the book Personalized organizes the work into five promises to the customer, which are to empower, to know, to reach, to show and to delight. Execution starts from a consumer need and a business objective, uses data of four types that often suffers from gaps, silos and staleness, and relies on a set of technologies for recognizing customers, gaining insight, deciding what to offer, delivering it and optimizing results. Organization matters more than technology, with roughly 70 percent of the effort going to people. Consumers resist aggressive early personalization, and companies that personalize well outgrow those that do not. The next-best-product, offer and action models are criticized as inside-out, so a next best experience approach is proposed instead.

## Concept

The book Personalized treats personalization as a compounding source of competitive advantage rather than a single marketing tactic, on the model of BCG's time-based competition. Value grows with the volume of customer interactions a company can learn from, n, multiplied by the square of the speed of learning, v, because faster experimentation compounds rather than simply accumulates. This personalization flywheel is hard for competitors to copy, since it is built from years of accumulated interaction data and learning velocity instead of a point solution. <span class="src">Personalized</span>

The framework has five sequential promises. Empower Me, described as the most important, uses everything known about a customer to help achieve a specific goal, by educating, helping them find or fix something or rewarding them, in the channel and moment they choose, and requires identifying which parts of the journey are worth personalizing instead of personalizing everything. Know Me is foundational and depends on structuring customer data by provenance, with zero-party data volunteered by the customer, first-party data inferred from the customer's recorded behavior and owned by the company, second-party data from a trusted partner under the customer's permission, such as a co-branded credit card, and third-party data bought or licensed from platforms and marketplaces, while loyalty-program data is unusually valuable because members are typically 2-3 times more engaged than average customers. It requires a data strategy that starts from target use cases, a customer data platform that stitches sources into one view, increasingly through probabilistic identity matching, and governance, typically owned by a chief data or privacy officer, that defines shared terms, audits quality and provenance and enforces red lines on data use. <span class="src">Personalized</span>

Reach Me turns knowledge into timely contact through three intelligence components. Targeting intelligence combines trigger events, such as an abandoned cart, with propensity, affinity and headroom models to decide when to act, experiment design and activation applies a rules layer of exclusions, frequency caps and business rules to decide which customers qualify for which experiences, with leaders running many multivariate experiments in parallel rather than sequential A/B tests, and next best action orchestration is a cross-channel air traffic control layer, sometimes a state machine that tracks each customer's progress through a journey, which prioritizes and sequences messages and suppresses conflicting ones, as by pausing promotions during a service incident. Show Me concerns content, which has shifted from one finished asset per interaction, too slow and limited for scale, toward modular templated components tagged as searchable metadata and increasingly generated and varied by tools such as Adobe Firefly and Jasper, up to atomic content that updates itself with real-time data after a message has been sent. Delight Me is the compounding layer, driven by a measurement loop that lets agile teams improve weekly or faster. <span class="src">Personalized</span>

Success depends on people and process more than technology, formalized as the 70/20/10 rule, in which about 70% of the effort in a successful AI and personalization transformation goes to people, meaning processes, ways of working and incentives, 20% to getting data right and 10% to technology. The book warns against starting by redesigning the org chart, since reorganization is slow and premature before the operational requirements are understood, and recommends small, cross-functional, self-governing pods with clear goals and decision rights, running two-week agile sprints on a narrow, high-visibility use case and evolving toward centralized functions only as the program scales. <span class="src">Personalized</span>

Measurement rests on three metrics, customer interaction engagement, such as open rates, conversion and browsing, broken out by customer type and channel, net incremental sales lift and long-term customer lifetime value. Lift is the hard one, because summing the incremental revenue claimed by overlapping campaigns double-counts effects, so best-in-class companies keep a universal control group that receives only non-personalized mass communication as a clean baseline, and all three must be measured near real time, since the learning cycle that drives advantage is the number of experiments times how quickly they can be measured and acted on, and waiting weeks breaks the loop. <span class="src">Personalized</span>

The book maps risks onto each promise with mitigations. Under Know Me the risks are unclear consent and mishandled or stranded data, met by elevated chief privacy or data officer roles, metadata tagging of permitted uses and customer visibility and control over data. Under Reach Me they are inappropriate targeting and message bombardment across teams, met by embedded rules engines with auditable dashboards, cross-team orchestration and human review of automated targeting for edge cases. Under Show Me they are outdated content, copyright exposure from generative output and bias, met by templated content with expiration and refresh rules, tying generated content to the company's own licensed library through embeddings for source verification and audit trails, and human review of generated batches, and under Delight Me the risk is harmful edge-case output, met by red-team testing that deliberately surfaces unusual scenarios, such as customers with atypical service-contact patterns, before they reach production. <span class="src">Personalized</span>

The authors' HBR article turns the promises into a scored diagnostic, the Personalization Index, built from 200 marketer interviews, mystery shopping of how companies deliver across channels and a financial analysis linking personalization maturity to growth and shareholder returns. It runs from 0 to 100, with Empower Me worth 50 points, Know Me, Reach Me, Show Me and Delight Me 10 each, and a final 10 for a senior leader with P&L accountability and a cross-functional mandate, a factor that often decides success on its own. Across 12 industry segments companies average only 49, with the top decile at 72, and in a BCG survey of 5,000 consumers two-thirds report a recent personalized recommendation that felt inappropriate, inaccurate or invasive although over 80% say they want personalization, a gap the authors attribute to lack of a clear standard for good personalization. While 85% of C-suite executives planned to raise 2024 AI spending, mostly on cost-saving, leaders grow 10 percentage points faster than laggards and 6 faster than average, with 30% higher customer spend and triple the pre- and post-purchase engagement, every 10-point Index rise correlates with about a 7-point rise in Net Promoter Score and USD 1 invested in a leader five years ago would be worth USD 3 against USD 0.50 for a laggard, implying a USD 2 trillion prize. <span class="src">Personalization Done Right</span>

Named examples illustrate each promise. SonderMind's mental-wellness app personalizes treatment journeys from an interactive 12-brain-function assessment, for Empower Me, Spotify's more than 600 million users and Sweetgreen's 60% digital sales share in 2023 show deep first-party relationships at very different scale, for Know Me, Cisco combines product-use, service, marketing and external financial-news data to tell its B2B sales team whom to contact and when, for Reach Me, Pandora used AI-generated, individually tailored imagery to cut some content cycles from 12-14 months to 10 days, for Show Me, and DoorDash runs hundreds of in-app micro-experiments, for Delight Me. Voya Financial's My Voyage app shows all five working together, unifying a customer's retirement, savings and healthcare benefits in one app with a test engine that experiments on which nudges help members, which grew accounts, produced double-digit revenue growth and earned recognition on Newsweek's Most Trusted Financial Services brands and Ethisphere's World's Most Ethical Companies lists. <span class="src">Personalization Done Right</span>

Three lessons separate leaders from laggards. Leadership must be rethought so the mandate comes explicitly from the CEO and every relevant C-suite function holds named accountability, the CFO for the business case, the CDO for digital experience, the CMO for agile marketing, the CDAO for data platforms, the COO for delivery processes, general counsel for responsible AI and the CIO for technology investment, rather than leaving personalization to marketing. The foundation is secured by using the Index to find gaps, tying investment to each, and favoring smart integration of existing tools over building everything, since programs can become largely self-funding once early wins prove the case, and companies must compete on speed, since leaders learn about ten times faster, compressing 8-12-week campaign cycles to three to five days, as a large US bank did on its second attempt with an empowered, budget-holding cross-functional pod after a first attempt created another silo, cutting its cycle from 12 weeks to 3 days and halving credit-card churn. <span class="src">Personalization Done Right</span>

David Edelman's 2022 article shows the same thinking earlier as intelligent experience engines, with five practices that map onto the later promises, connecting data signals into a 360-degree customer view, reimagining the end-to-end journey, activating experiences across channels, fulfilling according to customer context and testing relentlessly, and it already states the 70/20/10 ratio. Brinks Home, a struggling smart-home brand with 2% market share against ADT, Google Nest and Ring, worked with the AI start-up OfferFit to scale A/B testing from two or three a day to roughly 50,000, raising average direct-to-consumer package size from USD 489 to USD 968 and revenue by 9.5% within a year. Lululemon builds its 360-degree view from email capture at purchase, in-store yoga sign-ups, clickstream data and, after acquiring Mirror, in-home workout behavior, Qantas extends personalization into a cross-category loyalty ecosystem with partners such as Woolworths, Hilton and Avis and a health-insurance app that rewards healthy habits, Starbucks and the much smaller sweetgreen, with 140 stores against more than 33,000, both activate across channels, with sweetgreen's digital share higher, Kroger's 84.51° and Tesco's dunnhumby run hundreds of propensity models, and Stitch Fix runs hundreds of experiments a month expecting a third to fail. <span class="src">Customer Experience in the Age of AI</span>

PwC researchers supply the strategic rationale for the Know Me emphasis on proprietary data. Most of the customer data companies spend billions acquiring, market research, purchased data and generic big-data analysis, is available to competitors and so does not differentiate, whereas a system of privileged insights holds unique, trust-built information on needs, desires and experiences that only one company has. It rests on three linked practices, a foundation of trust and value, as Adobe refuses to use customer data for tactical advantage over customer benefit, integrating insight collection into everyday touchpoints, as Zara and Inditex train store employees as a frontline sensing network that feeds real-time style and inventory signals to headquarters and supports more than 60,000 designs a year, and wiring insights into operations, incentives and metrics, as in Salesforce's customer-success-driven product development, and it is a flywheel in which trust drives engagement, which yields insight, which improves the experience and builds more trust. <span class="src">How to Gain a Competitive Advantage on Customer Insights</span>

HBR's article on AI-driven companies asks what distinguishes the minority that scales, given that only 10% of companies have mastered scaling generative AI, per BCG research, while 70% of workers report enthusiasm. AI-driven companies embed AI in strategic priorities as a portfolio of real customer problems rather than pursuing efficiency alone, as one large consumer products company used AI efficiency savings to fund customer-value investment to signal that AI was a growth lever rather than a pretext for cuts, they break down functional silos with small two-pizza pods of business, analytics, tech and operations iterating against clear KPIs, and the same 10% are furthest ahead in scaling predictive AI because they had built the foundation of the Know Me layer, and they use AI to improve experience, as a large convenience retailer's AI scheduling tool raised employee satisfaction and lowered turnover by matching shift preferences and was rolled out nationally in six months against two-plus years for earlier scheduling tools because front-line teams saw that they personally benefited. <span class="src">What Sets AI-Driven Companies Apart</span>

Forrester's survey research shows a mismatch between corporate enthusiasm and consumer experience. Consumers often equate personalization with intrusive marketing, one participant describing it as coming with privacy violations and another wanting brands to leave them be, and resistance concentrates at the start of the relationship, since 54% of US online adults will not share personal information for more relevant advertising and only 27% want personalized emails, with individualized messaging facing most pushback in the discover, evaluate and commit stages. It softens once trust exists, as 42% consider personalized experiences an important part of loyalty programs, and comfort varies with sensitivity, since only 12% of people who recently opened a bank account want personalized banking advice against 28% willing to share information for a better retail experience. Companies that front-load aggressive personalization before any relationship exists work against consumer sentiment, and deeper personalization has to be earned through demonstrated value. <span class="src">The State Of Consumer Personalization, 2022</span>

Strategy comes first, and Forrester defines a spectrum of depth appropriate to a given moment, running from segmentation, bucketing customers into predefined groups, the oldest approach and still right for many uses, through discrete interactions, single-moment next-best-offer or next-best-action suggestions triggered by one action, to preset personas or customer-determined journeys, such as a beauty brand's dry-skin track or a car-seat maker's toddler stage that shapes an ongoing sequence of communication, up to contextual understanding that anticipates individual needs in real time, formalized as Next Best Experience and needing real-time interaction management. Strategy should be structured around four questions in order, what the consumer actually needs, grounded in research and not the justification that we can personalize, so we should, what the business objective and measurable KPI are, what the tactic is and where in the lifecycle it applies, and who needs to be involved, since personalization spans customer insights, data and analytics, CRM, IT and marketing, so everyone owns it and no single function does, an organizational paradox to resolve deliberately. <span class="src">A Strategy Guide to Consumer Personalization</span>

Data is the fuel and Forrester classes it on two axes. Provenance runs from zero-party data, the rarest and most valuable, proactively and explicitly volunteered, such as preference-center selections, purchase intentions and personal context, through first-party data a company collects directly, including transactions, demographics, behavior and implied interest, and second-party data from two companies combining customer sets under partnership, such as a financial-services firm and a retailer, to third-party purchased or licensed data, such as affinity or contextual data, which is falling out of favor as privacy regulation tightens. Frequency separates static data, such as birth date, gender and address, used to define audience segments, from dynamic data, such as location, temperature and energy use, which must be captured and acted on in near real time and triggers a specific personalized moment. Companies repeatedly hit three problems, missing data, which turns a quick test into a new collection project, siloed data, as when social media learns a preference that never reaches CRM or email, and non-actionable data, centrally aggregated but too stale, disjointed or unclean for real-time decisions, and the problems are intensifying, since 80% of B2C CMOs say rising consumer privacy protections are changing their view of what personalization can involve, up from 45% two quarters earlier. The recommended sequence is to identify the data a tactic needs, locate it, plan to access and activate it respecting owners' governance and verify its quality, since personalization based on unreliable or improperly matched unknown-person data is less accurate. <span class="src">A Data Primer for Consumer Personalization</span>

Execution also requires technology, though Forrester stresses that no single personalization category exists, since only 19% of B2C marketers using enterprise martech say one vendor supplies everything and the average program stitches together about eight technologies. Five functional categories must be covered. Recognition identifies who the customer is, through customer data platforms, zero-party-data and preference-center tools, identity resolution and journey mapping, and building a single cross-channel view remains among marketers' top execution challenges in Forrester's 2023 survey. Insights turns recognized data into understanding of journey stage, behavior and intent through customer, journey and digital-intelligence analytics. Decisioning produces the next-best-offer, action or experience, from simple rules to machine-learning models, real-time interaction management, creative adtech that assembles and tests ad variants and journey orchestration platforms that adjust a path in near real time. Delivery executes through advertising, cross-channel marketing, commerce and customer-service platforms across email, SMS, app, website, social, store and contact center, and optimization runs in the background through marketing resource management, measurement and experience-optimization platforms that measure customer-facing value, such as Net Promoter Score, and business value, such as incremental sales, sometimes using reinforcement learning to improve decisioning. <span class="src">A Technology Overview of Consumer Personalization</span>

Forrester's vendor checklist asks which of the five categories a tool supports, rather than what its marketing claims, what depth of personalization it can execute for the organization's data maturity and goals, what level of identity resolution it achieves, since segmentation does not need full resolution, how automated its decisioning really is, with a warning to be skeptical of overpromised AI because rules-based decisioning remains omnipresent under much AI marketing and human supervision of automation is still needed, how it measures and optimizes impact across customer-facing and company metrics, and how deeply it integrates with the CX, marketing, sales, commerce and service ecosystem, which is preferable but not always necessary. <span class="src">A Technology Overview of Consumer Personalization</span>

Next Best Experience begins from evidence that relationships have shifted, since a Verint study of more than 34,000 consumers worldwide found declining loyalty and retention, with two-thirds saying they would switch for a better customer experience, so price and product quality no longer protect on their own. The article argues that the dominant analytics paradigm, the next best trilogy of product, offer and action, has quietly failed because each was designed to optimize a business outcome rather than the customer's experience. Next best product used recommendation and cross-sell propensity models that raised average revenue per user while ignoring the wider relationship, next best offer tailored outbound campaigns to segments but, focused narrowly on campaigns, produced ad fatigue, and next best action, powered by AI and real-time interaction management, optimized for business context and anticipated needs yet failed to make customers feel valued and in some cases worsened the relationship, and the shared flaw is an inside-out perspective that is about what the business wants, presented as personalization. <span class="src">Marching toward the “Next Best Impact” model</span>

Next Best Experience instead recommends the ideal next interaction a customer should have with the brand, derived from signals across the full journey and used consistently across marketing, sales, support and finance, and unlike the trilogy it does not always recommend an action, since sometimes the best next experience is no interaction, a rejection of always-be-engaging logic. An effective program blends six practices, quality cross-touchpoint data so recommendations reflect the real journey, outcome-driven metrics such as customer lifetime value and headroom potential rather than campaign ROI, which systematically over-prioritizes already-engaged customers and under-prioritizes unengaged ones with untapped spending potential, understanding why a customer chooses a product or channel, their motivations and personality, not only what they will buy next, anticipating experience problems through contextual signals rather than waiting for feedback, a culture of experimentation that tolerates failed micro-experiments because a knowledge graph of preferences is built incrementally, and organizational redesign, which may reorganize departments and turn traditional roles into technology-oriented ones, to align marketing, sales, service and finance around consistent personalized experiences, on the premise that today's best experience becomes tomorrow's baseline, so the target keeps moving. <span class="src">Marching toward the “Next Best Impact” model</span>

## Open Questions

- The book's examples and case studies, including H&M, Spotify and various retailers and telcos, are not independently verified, and figures such as incremental revenue outcomes are the authors' consulting experience rather than audited results. <span class="src">Personalized</span>
- The sources do not explain why the 70/20/10 ratio and the five-practice structure persisted essentially unchanged across about three years of the same authors' work despite the acceleration of generative AI, so it is unclear whether this reflects stability or insufficient updating. <span class="src">Customer Experience in the Age of AI</span> <span class="src">Personalized</span>
- The AI-driven companies article does not say how the 10% scaling figure was measured or what threshold separates mastering scaling from not, which makes the selection of featured companies hard to verify. <span class="src">What Sets AI-Driven Companies Apart</span>
- The sources do not say how to sequence investment across the five technology categories on a limited budget, for example whether recognition should always precede the more visible decisioning layer, nor which roles and KPI structures would operationalize Next Best Experience day to day. <span class="src">A Technology Overview of Consumer Personalization</span> <span class="src">Marching toward the “Next Best Impact” model</span>
