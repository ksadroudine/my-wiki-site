## Summary

Telecom operators are repositioning themselves as AI companies because conventional efficiency programs and connectivity growth have left the sector trailing other industries in shareholder returns. The strategic case combines an AI-first reinvention of customer care, network operations and decision-making, which consultancies estimate could lift EBITDA by double-digit percentage points, with growth plays such as personalized consumer AI, enterprise convergence, sovereign AI infrastructure and partnerships with AI-native companies like Perplexity. Capturing that value depends less on algorithms than on scaling discipline, meaning end-to-end workflow redesign, a reusable AI factory platform, unified data, built-in change management, budget accountability and early involvement of risk functions, as shown by operators such as SK Telecom, KPN, AT&T and Singtel.

## Concept

Telecom operators face a structural investment problem that explains why AI is described as a strategic reset rather than a tool. Over the five years to 2023 the median telco's annualized total shareholder return improved only from 3% to 6%, which still ranked the sector 31st among 33 and well below the broader market's 13%. Eighty-five percent of the industry's net value creation came from dividends rather than growth in market capitalization, and more than 40% of operators ended the period smaller than they began it. Telcos also trade at enterprise multiples of about 5-7x, far below the roughly 20x that data-center and cloud providers command, a gap BCG attributes to investor doubt that operators can capture AI value without fundamental change. Conventional efficiency programs show diminishing returns and ARPU growth has proved elusive, so AI is positioned as the lever that could change both the cost base and the growth narrative. BCG's value-creator research places AI among seven ingredients that successful telcos combine, alongside defending the core, growing outside the core, maximizing asset leverage, radically optimizing costs, core-to-cloud transformation and next-generation network architecture. <span class="src">A New Formula for Success</span> <span class="src">Turning AI Disruption into Telcos’ Growth Engine</span>

The core of the AI-first agenda is the reinvention of two domains. In customer care, agents authenticate customers and detect intent to power an always-on omnichannel concierge, calls that need a human are supported by real-time scripts and coaching modeled on the best performers, and root-cause analysis of customer issues feeds insight across the company, which BCG estimates can raise productivity by 45-60%, far beyond what call-deflection chatbots achieve. In network operations, reusable agents span the technology stack, from sensor agents monitoring telemetry to agents that schedule field engineers and advise on site, coordinated by an agent orchestrator that runs diagnostic, root-cause and preventive agents, for an estimated 40-50% productivity gain. Smaller gains are available in IT and corporate functions, and the overall estimate is a 30-40% productivity improvement. Beyond productivity, hyper-personalized customer value management, dynamic pricing and network-as-a-service, which lets enterprises consume bandwidth as software, are expected to lift revenue by 2-3%, together with reduced revenue leakage from more completely billing enterprise value-added services, and growth levers account for roughly 30% of the projected EBITDA gain. Because running AI raises recurring costs, the net picture is that the operator keeps 70-90% of the benefit and earns a projected 20-35% five-year internal rate of return, provided the transformation is a coherent redesign. The productivity gains are open to any operator that executes well, but the revenue opportunities will be captured disproportionately by first movers in each market. <span class="src">The AI-First Telco</span> <span class="src">A New Formula for Success</span>

BCG's central instruction is to reshape rather than deploy. Value comes from connecting AI initiatives so that agents and assets are reused across the enterprise and an observe-plan-act cycle links key processes end to end, with an orchestrating role coordinating specialized agents on cross-functional tasks, whereas disconnected pilots remain isolated and capture little. The recommended starting point is a readiness check covering C-level and business alignment, a three-year investment horizon and clear profit-and-loss accountability for each major solution. The 10-20-70 rule expresses the same lesson quantitatively, directing only 10% of effort to algorithms and 20% to technology and data while 70% goes to people and operational transformation, and the data must be ready first because consistent agent performance depends on it. Some capability must also be built internally, since off-the-shelf products cannot always supply the business context, reusability and architectural fit an operator's workflows require. An operator's perspective from Singtel's chief AI, data and analytics officer reinforces this, arguing that the strongest opportunities combine material business value, proprietary data or context, and a workflow AI can meaningfully change, so that a great model in a weak process creates a demo while a good-enough model in the right workflow creates a business. He expects advantage to shift from model access to data, context, memory, tools and governance, and describes the leadership task as building the system that solves problems, focusing on a few non-negotiable outcomes with clear ownership, and treating intelligence as a reusable capability embedded in daily decisions. <span class="src">The AI-First Telco</span> <span class="src">A New Formula for Success</span> <span class="src">Turning AI Into Enterprise Value</span>

Beyond the core, BCG's strategic framework describes four plays that an operator can combine according to its ambition, assets and technical maturity. The AI-first core telco is the mandatory baseline, reshaping network operations, customer service and decision-making into a self-learning, self-healing system through predictive care, next-best-action management and closed-loop network automation, with estimated EBITDA gains of 5-15 percentage points within 12-18 months. AI monetization in consumer markets builds hyperpersonalized, AI-native experiences that deepen loyalty and lift ARPU, and SK Telecom's A. assistant, which has passed 10 million monthly active users, is the cited proof that such products attract real usage. AI convergence repositions the operator as an end-to-end enterprise digital partner rather than a connectivity vendor, as in Orange's combination of the Orange Cyberdefense acquisition, Google Cloud and Microsoft partnerships and the Orange Business managed-services platform. The sovereign AI infrastructure play uses operators' regulatory relationships and physical footprint to build AI-ready data centers, GPU-as-a-service platforms and compliance-grade connectivity for governments and enterprises, as with Singtel's Nxera brand and Nvidia-partnered GPU service. Four trends make these choices urgent, namely AI becoming the network's control system, data-center and sovereign cloud capacity becoming the backbone of computing, hyperscaler competition in programmable network services, and on-device AI assistants that could disintermediate operators from their own customers. AI talent and an AI-first culture underlie all four plays. <span class="src">Turning AI Disruption into Telcos’ Growth Engine</span>

SK Telecom shows how an operator can pursue the AI-company agenda through internal build and external alliances at once. It plans to triple the share of spend devoted to AI from 12% to 33% by 2028 and to reach USD 18.5 billion in AI revenue, up from USD 13 billion in 2022, so that AI makes up about a third of revenue. Its AI Pyramid Strategy has an infrastructure layer of data centers, AI semiconductors through its Sapeon subsidiary and a multilingual telecom language model, a transformation layer that applies AI across mobile, broadband, enterprise and new verticals such as healthcare and mobility with mid-term goals of cutting service costs by 20-30%, and a service layer led by the A. chatbot assistant launched in 2022 and enhanced with Microsoft Azure's OpenAI service. Alongside this build-out, SK Telecom has invested in Anthropic, Allganize and Imprimed, formed a 16-company domestic K-AI Alliance, and joined a Global AI Alliance with Deutsche Telekom, e& and Singtel to develop telecom-specific language models and AI platforms. Deutsche Telekom and SK Telecom are also working with OpenAI, Google and Meta on a telecom-specific model to power a next-generation digital customer assistant. <span class="src">AI Native Telco Journey of SK Telecom</span> <span class="src">A New Formula for Success</span>

A second, more visible pattern is partnership with AI-native companies, exemplified by Perplexity. In 2024 SK Telecom invested USD 10 million to co-develop language models and integrate Perplexity's search into A., and Deutsche Telekom and SoftBank followed within months, offering Perplexity to a combined mobile and broadband base of more than 335 million users. By 2025 the arrangements had deepened from bundling free premium subscriptions into co-development, including SoftBank's plan for an AI-native phone priced under USD 1,000. Operators justify the model through better customer engagement than traditional voice assistants, subscription revenue as legacy margins compress, first-mover differentiation, AI-native devices and access to an ecosystem built around a well-funded challenger to larger Silicon Valley providers, and proponents frame it as operators positioning themselves as AI companies rather than simply buying AI tools. Telecom-focused generative AI also reaches sales and IT, where personalized sales emails generated from customer data free representatives for customer-facing time and coding assistants produce code, documentation and automated tests. <span class="src">Telcos Are Becoming AI Companies with Perplexity</span> <span class="src">Why Telecom Operators Are Flocking to Perplexity</span> <span class="src">A New Formula for Success</span>

The binding constraint on all of these plays is scaling, and McKinsey's surveys document a persistent gap between adoption and value. Reported impact roughly doubled in a year, from about 25% to about 50% of executives capturing impact from AI and generative AI, but a later survey found only 12% had captured sizable impact, with most stuck in what the research calls the money step, where investment and enthusiasm have not yet reached the balance sheet. The explanation lies mainly in how operators deploy the technology, since nearly three-quarters use AI to support only minimal or moderate redesigns of existing processes and so accelerate legacy ways of working. Redesigning workflows from scratch around the necessary steps instead eliminates whole stages, handoffs and coordination layers, which is why genuine redesign separates sustained gains in cost, cycle time and quality from AI that merely shifts a bottleneck. <span class="src">Scaling the AI-native telco</span> <span class="src">Telcos’ AI inflection point</span>

Seven elements recur as the difference between operators that scale and those that stay in pilots. The first is to target end-to-end domain workflows rather than single use cases, since one personalization use case rarely moves churn, whereas one operator that combined audiencing, a unified churn model, real-time next-best-action and automated testing achieved the lowest churn in its national market. The second is a centralized, modular AI platform of reusable services, with which one North American operator built about fifty reusable services and cut new-use-case build time from months to roughly two weeks and avoids duplication such as customer-care and network teams separately building near-identical knowledge tools. The third is unified data foundations spanning structured and unstructured material, because data limitations are cited by 30% of executives as a current inhibitor and by 45% as the expected inhibitor for scaling agents. The fourth is change management designed into each use case from the outset, necessary in organizations whose employees stay 7.5 years on average, and the fifth is a joint operating model and talent strategy, since about 85% of operators report AI decisions made by disconnected teams. The sixth is a genuine partner ecosystem that treats vendors as partners, and the seventh is deliberate management of regulatory and ethical risk through guardrails and a responsible-AI framework, on the logic that trust determines whether operators can monetize their customer and network data. <span class="src">Scaling the AI-native telco</span>

The same research sorts AI agents into five architectural forms that help leaders scope what a use case calls for. Simple reflex agents act on preset rules, like current next-best-action systems that warn a customer nearing a data limit, and utility-based agents weigh several options in context, for example deciding whether to auto-reply to an unhappy customer or draft a response for a person. Goal-based agents work toward an explicit target such as an upsell revenue goal, hierarchical agents decompose a complex task into ordered subtasks, and multi-agent systems have several agents work independently or cooperatively toward a shared objective, such as a centralized employee assistant that answers queries and completes field notes across network tasks. <span class="src">Scaling the AI-native telco</span>

To make scaling repeatable, McKinsey proposes an AI factory, a common playbook of reusable platforms, tools and reference architectures including shared agent frameworks, model environments and standard governance controls. Without it, teams adopt different tools and patterns, which raises technical debt and infrastructure costs and creates security, compliance and data-governance risk. A factory includes a structured intake process that assesses candidate use cases, in some operators with an agent that recommends whether each should proceed to proof of concept, scale or be deprioritized, and a library of reusable components such as propensity-model blueprints. Workforce and accountability follow the same logic. About 30% of telecom working hours could be automated by 2030, but 91% of sector jobs will need fundamental redesign, so the dominant effect is role redefinition with new roles such as AI workflow architect and AI risk manager rising as traditional DevOps and customer-support roles decline. Leading operators also tie AI initiatives to budget lines and adjust divisional budgets to enforce delivery, on the principle that if AI is not in the budget, it is not real. <span class="src">Telcos’ AI inflection point</span>

Capital allocation shows the scaling logic applied to a single, expensive domain. Telco capital expenditure has outgrown revenue for a decade, with invested capital up 77% in the US and 23% globally, while data-traffic growth is slowing from over 30% toward 10% in some markets, which opens a path to lower capital intensity only if operators know which investments actually improve the customer-perceived network experience. Simulating the customer-satisfaction effect of specific interventions such as new sites, spectrum additions and hardware upgrades, starting from a baseline of how satisfaction deteriorates without investment, has let operators optimize capital plans by 10-15%, repurpose up to 25% of planned spend and improve ROI within as little as 12 months. One operator that simulated more than 1,000 planned interventions found a long tail, over 10% of its capital plan, that would move satisfaction only about one-seventh as much as its best interventions, savings that engineering thresholds had missed. The approach depends on customer-experience scores as an input, careful scoping because of the compute cost of simulation, and governance that involves the CEO and CFO rather than the technology function alone. <span class="src">Pushing telcos' AI envelope on capital decisions</span>

Two operator cases show the scaling elements in practice. KPN built an agentic voice-AI customer-care system for roughly five million calls a year with McKinsey and QuantumBlack, using a custom language model to mine call transcripts and chats for high-impact, low-risk use cases such as customer verification, order status and technician scheduling, while keeping humans in moments such as churn-risk house moves. It designed for sub-two-second response latency and natural interruption, built a reusable three-layer architecture of agent orchestration, agent platform and audio integration connected to core systems through standard APIs and MCP, reviewed up to 100 real call transcripts each morning and shipped prompt fixes that evening, and trained employees as roles shifted, reaching an 86% adoption success rate and a target of agentic handling of 10-20% of service calls by 2027. <span class="src">How KPN is building an agentic AI engine for customer care</span>

AT&T illustrates governance and return discipline at much larger scale, with over 100,000 employees on its Ask AT&T generative and agentic platform generating tens of billions of tokens a day. Its chief data and AI officer credits scaling to three practices, namely formal ROI cases for every use case with resources concentrated on the highest-value ones, embedding AI in redesigned workflows rather than layering it onto legacy processes, and involving risk organizations early, because peers have failed when risk teams were brought in late. AT&T also practices iterative AI-fueled coding, which it contrasts with one-shot vibe coding, which its chief data and AI officer calls the death spiral of an enterprise. The practice has cut the time to build a governed data product from four to six weeks to as little as 30 minutes, sometimes with no changes at architect review. A sandboxed Permit to Learn environment lets employees try agentic workflows on real data without touching production, and a Level Up program supports developer education, which shows that adoption needs engineered enablement as well as capable technology. <span class="src">Forward-looking leadership How this telecom giant puts AI into action</span>

## Open Questions

- BCG's productivity and EBITDA figures are consulting estimates without fully disclosed methodology, including the assumptions behind the 70-90% net-benefit retention range. <span class="src">The AI-First Telco</span>
- The sources do not show how many of the 59 telcos in the value-creators study are actually pursuing the generative AI ingredient, so sector-wide progress toward the AI-first model is hard to judge. <span class="src">A New Formula for Success</span>
- The sources report SK Telecom's targets and partnership announcements but not independently audited progress against its 2028 revenue and cost targets, and they do not establish how deep or exclusive the Perplexity partnerships are relative to operators' other AI vendor ties. <span class="src">AI Native Telco Journey of SK Telecom</span> <span class="src">Telcos Are Becoming AI Companies with Perplexity</span>
- No source gives the cost of building an AI factory, a KPN-style agent platform or a capex-simulation capability, so the return on those platforms cannot be compared with the savings they generate. <span class="src">Telcos’ AI inflection point</span> <span class="src">How KPN is building an agentic AI engine for customer care</span> <span class="src">Pushing telcos' AI envelope on capital decisions</span>
