## Summary

AI speeds up coding, but gains stall at review, governance and decision bottlenecks unless the whole development system is redesigned. Bain, McKinsey and an MIT study agree that machine-readable knowledge, automated verification, new roles and faster decision rights separate leaders from the rest.

## Highlights

- An MIT Sloan study of more than 100,000 GitHub developers found AI tools raised coding activity by up to 180% cumulatively (autocomplete 40%, synchronous agents 140%, asynchronous agents 180%), yet produced only 50% more projects and 30% more releases, with no matching rise in app downloads or reviews. <span class="src">AI boosts worker productivity — but does that translate to final outputs?</span>
- Bain's survey of 293 technology leaders shows developers completing about 21% more tasks while review time rises roughly 91%, so leaders expect 95% productivity uplift against the 20-27% gains most capture today. <span class="src">The Missing Architecture for Agentic Software Development</span>
- Bain names three parts of the missing architecture — knowledge agents can consume, a deterministic quality harness that "automates confidence," and a software development life cycle managed as a product — and finds safety, risk and governance guardrails (43%) the top enabler for scaling agents. <span class="src">The Missing Architecture for Agentic Software Development</span>
- McKinsey's survey of 334 product and engineering leaders finds only 25% of directors and above report meaningful AI acceleration while 30% report productivity falling, and that teams that both embedded AI and redesigned roles ("transformers") became top accelerators 40% of the time versus 27% for adopters and 17% for experimenters. <span class="src">Scaling the agentic product development life cycle</span>
- Top accelerators shrank squads (median from about ten to seven people), split roles into definers who fuse product and design and builders who fuse development, testing and operations, and write specifications precise enough for an agent to act on and a test suite to check. <span class="src">Scaling the agentic product development life cycle</span>
- Bain's survey of software companies shows pyramid-shaped engineering organizations falling from 66% to 29%, developers expecting to spend a third of their time directing agents within two years, and 70% of companies still deciding centrally, so the faster code stalls at unchanged approvals. <span class="src">The Half-Finished Redesign How AI Reshapes Software Organizations</span>
- QuantumBlack rebuilt its design system as AI-native infrastructure on shadcn and Tailwind, arguing that when anyone can build, explicit design intent, tokens and agent skills scale judgment and make AI output cheaper and more consistent. <span class="src">When everyone can build, design is the differentiator</span>

## Concept

The sources describe one pattern from several angles, which Bain calls the shifting bottleneck paradox. AI collapses the cost of writing code, so the constraint moves to review, verification, release approval and decision-making. The MIT study traces this stage by stage through writing, committing, pull requests, merging and releasing, finding productivity effects attenuated at each later human step, and recommends smaller teams, reallocating staff to merge, release and maintenance work, and using AI to ease downstream constraints rather than only accelerating one stage. <span class="src">AI boosts worker productivity — but does that translate to final outputs?</span>

Bain's architecture article treats the surrounding system as the unit of optimization. Engineering knowledge, which usually lives in senior engineers' heads, has to be captured in structured specifications, conventions and escalation paths so agents inherit it once in writing (Stripe's "minions" each carry a spec covering objective, scope, context, verification and constraints, and merge about 1,300 AI-authored pull requests a week under normal human review). Verification has to be automated through risk scoring of each change's blast radius, policy-as-code guardrails, evaluation frameworks and audit trails, with humans reserved for exceptions; 41% of surveyed companies expect a risk-tiered model and only 6% fully autonomous development. The development life cycle itself needs an owner, roadmap and metrics that go beyond DORA measures to separate human and agent contributions and treat risk as a scorecard dimension. Amazon (up to 70% of code reviews handled by AI) and Craft Docs (from 15-20 to over 100 issues a week) are cited as examples. <span class="src">The Missing Architecture for Agentic Software Development</span>

McKinsey's product-development study adds the evidence that role redesign, not tools, separates leaders. Around 80% of surveyed engineers report average AI acceleration near 3% while the top 20% report 55%, and closing that gap is estimated at about $0.8 trillion. Leaders redesign end-to-end workflows around asynchronous agent "night shifts" (Sonar cut pull-request cycle time 3.4 times), give agents scoped mandates and escalation paths, build a shared AI operations layer for identity, permissions, evaluation, logging and cost, triage verification findings by risk, and run adoption as an embedded-coaching change program tracked by outcome metrics rather than licenses. Warning signs are that average time savings (11.8%) outrun rework reduction (6.2%), and external research cited shows 45% of AI-generated code samples failing security checks. Token, compute and AI-operations spending can reach 20% of existing labor costs. McKinsey offers progress markers such as 70% of job descriptions changed, squads of two to four people and two-day sprints. <span class="src">Scaling the agentic product development life cycle</span>

Bain's organizational study explains why roles change faster than power. Teams have shrunk to pods of three to five, AI fluency is the leading hiring criterion (62%, three times raw coding skill), and junior engineers have fallen from a third of teams to under a fifth, removing the routine work on which judgment was once built. The decision layer is the skipped change: gains stall because approvals, handoffs and escalations are unchanged, so Bain advises distributing decision rights and defining where humans decide, where agents act and who owns outcomes, as one AI-native company did by letting feature engineers launch their own marketing. <span class="src">The Half-Finished Redesign How AI Reshapes Software Organizations</span>

QuantumBlack's design-system project shows the same principles in a single discipline. Its rebuild favours foundations AI already understands, composition over configuration, richer structure over exhaustive documentation, and room for both quick prototyping and production work, and it packages agent guidance alongside tokens and components so agents inherit conventions instead of relying on handcrafted prompts. <span class="src">When everyone can build, design is the differentiator</span>

## Open Questions

- Bain's expected uplifts (95% productivity, 148% release speed) are survey expectations rather than measured outcomes, and the McKinsey and Bain figures use different definitions of productivity, so they cannot be compared directly.
- The MIT study measures GitHub activity and app-store outcomes, which may not capture internal enterprise software where release does not depend on market adoption.
- Several examples (Stripe, Amazon, Sonar, the global bank) are company-reported, and the sources do not indicate how representative they are.
- The sources do not settle how much junior-engineer apprenticeship can be preserved while routine work is automated.
