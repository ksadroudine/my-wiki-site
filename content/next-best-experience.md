## Summary

A customer-analytics model arguing that the "next best trilogy" — next-best-product, next-best-offer, next-best-action — is an inside-out, marketing-ROI-driven approach. It should be replaced by a customer-centric "next best experience" built on outcome metrics and anticipatory, contextual interactions.

## Concept

The argument for Next Best Experience starts from evidence that customer relationships have shifted: a Verint study of over 34,000 consumers worldwide found declining loyalty and retention industry-wide, with two-thirds of respondents saying they would switch to a competitor purely on the basis of a better customer experience — meaning price and product quality, while still relevant, no longer function as durable competitive moats on their own. Against this backdrop, the article argues that the dominant customer-analytics paradigm — the "next best trilogy" of next best product, next best offer, and next best action — has quietly failed despite widespread adoption, because each of the three was designed to optimize a business outcome (cross-sell revenue, campaign engagement, real-time interaction efficiency) rather than the customer's actual experience. Next best product relied on recommendation and cross-sell propensity models that lifted average revenue per user but ignored the broader relationship; next best offer tailored outbound campaigns to different customer segments but, focused narrowly on campaigns, produced ad fatigue; next best action, powered by AI and real-time interaction management (RTIM), optimized for business context and anticipated customer needs but still failed to make customers feel genuinely valued — in the article's assessment, actually worsening the brand relationship in some cases. The shared flaw across all three is described as an "inside-out perspective": each is fundamentally about what the business wants from the customer, dressed up as personalization. <span class="src">Marching toward the “Next Best Impact” model</span>

Next Best Experience (NBX) is proposed as the replacement paradigm: rather than recommending the next product, offer, or action a business should push, NBX recommends the ideal next *interaction* a customer should have with the brand, derived from signals gathered across the customer's full journey and used consistently across marketing, sales, customer support, and finance rather than siloed within any one function. A notable structural difference from the trilogy models is that NBX does not necessarily recommend an action at every touchpoint — sometimes the best "next" experience is no interaction at all, which is a direct rejection of the always-be-engaging logic underlying next best action systems. An effective NBX program is described as a blend of six practices: collecting and analyzing quality customer data across all touchpoints so recommendations are grounded in the actual customer journey rather than a single channel's view; shifting to outcome-driven engagement metrics like customer lifetime value and headroom potential instead of campaign ROI or marketing spend, specifically because ROI-based metrics systematically over-prioritize customers who are already engaged and under-prioritize unengaged customers with significant untapped spending potential; understanding *why* a customer chooses a given product or channel (their underlying motivations and personality) rather than just predicting *what* they'll buy next, since the "why" is described as a more advanced and durable signal than next-purchase prediction; anticipating customer experience problems through contextual signals gathered at different journey touchpoints, rather than waiting for customer feedback to surface issues after the fact; deliberately building a culture of experimentation, including tolerance for failed micro-experiments, on the theory that a knowledge graph of customer preferences is best built incrementally through trial and error; and, finally, redefining organizational roles and change management, since delivering NBX consistently may require reorganizing departments and morphing some traditional roles into new technology-oriented ones. The stated goal is to align previously siloed teams (marketing, sales, service, finance) around a single shared mission of delivering consistent, personalized experiences, on the premise that today's best experience simply becomes tomorrow's baseline expectation, so the target is a continuously moving one rather than a fixed state to be achieved once. <span class="src">Marching toward the “Next Best Impact” model</span>
