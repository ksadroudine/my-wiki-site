## Summary

How telecom operators are repositioning themselves as AI companies, through layered internal AI strategies, external partnerships with AI providers like Perplexity, and a general four-play strategic framework spanning core operations, B2C monetization, enterprise convergence, and sovereign AI infrastructure.

## Concept

Telecom operators are increasingly describing their own transformation in terms of becoming AI companies rather than merely adopting AI as a tool, and are pursuing this through a combination of internal capability-building and external partnership. SK Telecom's stated strategy illustrates the internal-build approach: it is tripling the share of spend allocated to AI (from 12% to 33% by 2028) with a revenue target of USD 18.5 billion in AI revenue by 2028, up from USD 13 billion in 2022, aiming for AI to constitute roughly a third of total company revenue. This is organized as an "AI Pyramid Strategy" with three layers. The base layer, AI Infrastructure, covers data centers, AI semiconductors (developed through its Sapeon subsidiary to compete with NVIDIA and AMD in AI chipsets), and a multi-lingual large language model tailored to telecom data, built in part on SKT's Titan supercomputer and targeting proficiency in English, Japanese, and Spanish. The middle layer, AI Transformation (AIX), applies AI within SKT's existing mobile, broadband, and enterprise businesses as well as newer verticals such as healthcare and mobility, with mid-term goals including cutting service costs 20–30%; cited applications range from faster network equipment fault analysis to AI-assisted veterinary X-ray diagnosis and industrial safety monitoring. The top layer, AI Service, centers on "A." (A dot), an AI chatbot assistant launched in 2022 that has grown to serve over a million users for tasks like music, navigation, and messaging, enhanced via Microsoft Azure's OpenAI service and aiming for features like real-time translation. <span class="src">AI Native Telco Journey of SK Telecom</span>

SK Telecom pairs this internal build-out with external partnership and investment: it has invested directly in AI startups including Anthropic (multi-lingual LLMs), Allganize (enterprise AI), and Imprimed (cancer treatment prediction); formed the "K-AI Alliance," a group of 16 Korean AI startups spanning self-driving, robotics, chatbots, and manufacturing AI; and joined a Global AI Alliance with Deutsche Telekom, e&, and Singtel aimed at accelerating AI-driven transformation and jointly developing a Telco AI platform, including a planned telco-specific LLM and personal AI assistant for roaming services with Deutsche Telekom. <span class="src">AI Native Telco Journey of SK Telecom</span>

A parallel and more externally visible pattern is direct partnership with AI-native companies, exemplified by telecom operators' relationships with Perplexity. The pattern began in mid-2024, when SK Telecom invested $10 million in Perplexity specifically to collaborate on developing large language models and to integrate Perplexity's AI-powered search into its own "A." (Adot) personal assistant application; Deutsche Telekom and SoftBank followed within months, jointly offering Perplexity's capabilities to a combined mobile and broadband user base exceeding 335 million, explicitly framed as advancing both companies' stated mission of making advanced technology widely accessible. At this early stage, operators cited the value of the partnership in terms of enhanced user experience relative to traditional search, increased subscriber value from offering Perplexity's premium tier for free, and competitive differentiation from developing sophisticated AI personal assistants ahead of rivals still limited to conventional voice assistants. By 2025 that model had spread and deepened: SoftBank and Deutsche Telekom moved beyond simply bundling free Perplexity subscriptions into co-development arrangements, including SoftBank's plan for an "AI-Phone" under $1,000 built through product co-development. Operators frame the value of these deepened partnerships in terms of AI-powered customer engagement replacing traditional voice assistants, new subscription-based revenue streams as legacy telecom margins compress, competitive first-mover differentiation, joint development of AI-native devices, and access to a broader AI ecosystem through Perplexity's position as a well-funded challenger to larger Silicon Valley AI providers. The framing used by proponents of this model is that telecom operators partnering with an AI company are not simply purchasing AI tools, but positioning themselves as AI companies in their own right. <span class="src">Telcos Are Becoming AI Companies with Perplexity</span>

<span class="src">Turning AI Disruption into Telcos’ Growth Engine</span> (BCG, January 2026) supplies the general strategic framework this concept's case studies otherwise leave implicit: what, exactly, are telcos choosing between when they "become AI companies"? The article opens by diagnosing why the shift matters financially — telcos have lagged nearly every other technology sector in revenue growth and shareholder return over the past five years, and trade at enterprise multiples of just 5 to 7, far below the roughly 20x multiples data-center and cloud providers command, a valuation gap BCG attributes to investor doubt that telcos can capture value from AI without fundamental change. Four structural trends are reshaping the competitive landscape simultaneously: AI is becoming the network's control system (autonomous, self-optimizing traffic and failure prediction); data centers, AI clusters, and sovereign cloud programs (a market growing near 10% annually toward $400 billion by 2028) are becoming the backbone of global computing capacity, increasingly shaped by data-sovereignty regulation across 100+ measures in 40+ countries; network-as-a-service and API monetization is emerging as hyperscalers like AWS and Microsoft build competing programmable-connectivity offerings; and on-device AI assistants (projected on nearly three-quarters of Android devices by 2026) are becoming new gatekeepers mediating customer attention, threatening to disintermediate telcos from their own customer relationships. Against these trends, the article proposes four strategic plays telcos can combine based on ambition, assets, and technical maturity. "AI-First Core Telco" is framed as the mandatory baseline play regardless of which others a telco pursues: reshaping network operations, customer service, and decision-making into a leaner, self-learning, self-healing system via predictive care, next-best-action customer management, and closed-loop network automation, with BCG's analysis showing realizable EBITDA gains of 5 to 15 percentage points within 12 to 18 months. "AI Monetization in B2C" targets hyperpersonalized, AI-native consumer experiences that deepen loyalty and lift ARPU — SK Telecom's "A." assistant, also covered in this concept's other sources, is cited here as having surpassed 10 million monthly active users, evidence the strategy converts into real usage at scale. "AI Convergence Telco" repositions the operator as an end-to-end enterprise digital partner rather than a raw-connectivity vendor, targeting a projected $400+ billion enterprise AI spending market by 2027 through AI-optimized connectivity, industry-specific solutions, and cybersecurity AI — illustrated by Orange's combination of the Orange Cyberdefense acquisition, deep Google Cloud and Microsoft partnerships, and the Orange Business managed-services platform. "Sovereign AI Infrastructure Partner" leverages telcos' existing regulatory relationships, national infrastructure role, and physical footprint to build AI-ready data centers, GPU-as-a-Service platforms, and compliance-grade connectivity for governments and enterprises — Singapore's Singtel, also covered elsewhere in this concept, is cited here specifically for its Nxera sovereign-AI-hub brand and Nvidia-partnered GPUaaS offering. The article closes by naming AI talent acquisition and an AI-first internal culture as prerequisites underlying all four plays, not just the mandatory first one, and frames the choice among plays as a three-dimensional fit assessment across market context, ambition level, and existing assets and capabilities. <span class="src">Turning AI Disruption into Telcos’ Growth Engine</span>

## Open Questions

- Sources describe SK Telecom's targets and partnership announcements but do not report independently audited progress against the 2028 revenue and cost-reduction targets.
- It is not established from these sources how exclusive or how deep the Perplexity partnerships are relative to operators' other AI vendor relationships (e.g. SK Telecom's own OpenAI and Anthropic ties).
