## Concept

Telecom operators are pursuing quantum computing along two largely separate tracks: using quantum techniques to improve network performance, and preparing network security against the future threat that quantum computers pose to current encryption standards. On the performance side, SoftBank demonstrated in a 2025 proof of concept that an Ising machine — a quantum computing architecture suited to combinatorial optimization problems — could be used to fine-tune radio access network (RAN) base station settings, yielding a measured 10% increase in 5G downlink speed and up to 50% additional data capacity through carrier aggregation. SoftBank has stated an intent to extend this beyond a one-off optimization into broader network architecture and operational efficiency gains. Other major operators, including Vodafone, BT Group, SK Telecom, and Verizon, are exploring quantum computing for their own network use cases, though with less publicly detailed results as of mid-2025. <span class="src">Quantum Computing in Telecom Picks Up Pace in 2025</span>

On the security side, the concern is that sufficiently powerful quantum computers will eventually be able to break the public-key cryptography that underpins today's network security, which has prompted a distinct investment category: quantum-safe networking. This spans Quantum Key Distribution (QKD), which uses quantum mechanical properties to detect eavesdropping on a key exchange, and Post-Quantum Cryptography (PQC), classical cryptographic algorithms designed to resist attack by a quantum computer. An industry survey (the DSP Leaders' "Quantum-Safe Networking Market Perception Report," covered by TelecomTV) found that 7 of the top 10 organizations leading in this area are European, and that most surveyed telecom operators intend to have quantum-safe measures in place within four years — indicating this is being treated as a near-term operational priority rather than a distant, speculative concern. <span class="src">Quantum Computing in Telecom Picks Up Pace in 2025</span>
