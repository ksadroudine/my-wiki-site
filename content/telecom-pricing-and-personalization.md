## Summary

Telecom operators are squeezed by slow revenue growth, rising costs and commoditized pricing, and two complementary responses can restore their ability to earn more from customers. PwC's five Cs of convergence, customer experience, commercial innovation, cost excellence and complexity reduction target pricing power at the strategic level, while AI-driven personalization uses the customer, billing and network data operators already hold to create new revenue through targeted offers, real-time promotions and personalized billing. The personalization argument depends on a technology stack of a unified customer graph, real-time charging, event detection and next-best-action engines, but the sources supply no documented deployment and do not address the regulatory and trust risks of monetizing behavioral data.

## Concept

Telecom operators face a two-sided profitability squeeze. On the revenue side, PwC projects industry growth of only 2.9% a year globally from 2023 to 2028, held back by slowing macroeconomic growth, saturation in core connectivity and uncertainty about how quickly newer streams such as 5G services and B2B IoT will materialize. On the cost side, energy and labor costs are rising, and a widening East-West technology supply-chain divide reduces the scale efficiencies that equipment vendors can offer. Research across more than 50 national mobile markets confirms the commoditization pressure, since ARPU is declining in real terms in most markets, pricing differentiation between operators remains historically low, particularly in Europe despite a slight recent improvement, and the number of competing operators shows surprisingly little correlation with how differentiated pricing is. That last finding challenges the long-standing regulatory assumption that a particular number of mobile players determines competitive intensity, and it bears on whether regulators should reconsider restrictions on in-market consolidation. <span class="src">Five commercial imperatives to help telcos regain an edge</span>

The financial consequence is stark. Major US and EU mobile carriers generated USD 171 billion of operating cash from 2019 through 2023, of which USD 98 billion went to capital expenditure and acquisitions and USD 72 billion to debt service and dividends, leaving almost nothing for innovation, even though years of costly 5G investment have largely failed to lift inflation-adjusted ARPU in most markets. PwC therefore organizes its advice for telecom chief executives around rebuilding pricing power, meaning the ability to influence what the company charges, as distinct from only managing costs or waiting for market conditions to improve. <span class="src">Five commercial imperatives to help telcos regain an edge</span>

The first C, convergence, means bundling fixed, mobile and other services such as television into a single contract instead of separate contracts per connectivity type. It reinforces connectivity's status as a non-discretionary purchase, since consumers keep paying for gas, food and power even as prices rise, and it increases share of wallet, deepens loyalty, extends customer lifetime value and reduces churn because customers with several bundled services are stickier. The second C, customer experience and service excellence, uses AI to reverse telecom's poor service reputation, first by avoiding incidents and outages through better predictive maintenance and then by cutting the time and friction of recovery, as in AI-supported call-center agents. Charter Communications' chief executive describes the tools as helping agents get to a better answer faster and show more empathy, with expected gains in customer and employee satisfaction, fewer costly service visits and lower churn. <span class="src">Five commercial imperatives to help telcos regain an edge</span>

The third C, commercial innovation, enriches bundles beyond basic convergence by embedding often third-party elements such as content, insurance or segment-specific packages, for example a bundle of data, international calls and public-transit tickets for short-term visitors. Verizon's myPlan lets postpaid customers add or remove premium components such as video on demand and gaming for a fixed monthly fee, moving from a rigid one-size-fits-all bundle to a customizable subscription that strengthened its competitive position. The fourth C, cost excellence, covers classic levers such as lowering average labor cost through global delivery models and centers of excellence in lower-cost regions, reducing headcount through automation and AI that now extend from back-office work into customer-facing functions and even core network and software operations, and demand management and strategic sourcing on external spend. Vodafone's VOIS centralized back-office and technology functions into global shared-services hubs, and Rakuten Mobile's fully virtualized, cloud-native, AI-automated network cut both capital and operating expense by minimizing reliance on proprietary hardware, showing cost excellence as a competitive differentiator and not merely a defensive measure. <span class="src">Five commercial imperatives to help telcos regain an edge</span>

The fifth C, complexity reduction, targets a more structural cost, the variety of products, platforms and technology accumulated over decades, which compounds cost and slows the organization even when each item is well managed. PwC recommends legacy retirement, described as challenging and unglamorous but necessary to escape a self-perpetuating cost spiral, guided by a technology-architecture blueprint that decides which components to retire or modernize and favors modular, reusable ones. A large continental European mobile operator brought product and technology teams together in an agile, sprint-based program to retire legacy network assets, product platforms and IT systems, which took three years but produced internal and external cost reductions that more than justified the effort. <span class="src">Five commercial imperatives to help telcos regain an edge</span>

Personalization offers a second route to earning more from customers by building new revenue models rather than defending price. Consumer platforms such as Instagram, TikTok and Google sustain engagement with algorithms that learn each user's moods, hobbies, intentions and emotional state, and the proposal is that operators sit on an equivalent or richer data asset of billing history, device data, real-time network telemetry and support interactions that could be used the same way, not merely to improve service but to construct new revenue streams. <span class="src">How Telcos Can Monetize Personalization with AI</span>

Five mechanisms each map a type of customer signal to a commercial action. Add-on campaigns respond to behavioral patterns with real-time offers, such as a roaming pack for a frequent traveler or a tailored streaming bundle for a heavy streamer. Cross-sell and up-sell extends to smart devices, gaming add-ons, home automation and converged TV, mobile and broadband offers chosen from observed habits rather than a generic catalog. Event-driven promotions fire on momentary conditions, such as a low-balance top-up nudge or a discount when a customer is near a partner retailer, and need the system to act within a perishable window of relevance. Personalized billing pairs usage insights with loyalty discounts to build trust while raising ARPU, on the logic that a fair, individually relevant billing experience is itself a retention lever. Next-best-action recommendations anticipate needs such as a retention plan or upgrade before the customer asks, an approach whose limits are critiqued in [Customer Personalization Strategy and Execution](#/concept/customer-personalization-strategy). <span class="src">How Telcos Can Monetize Personalization with AI</span>

Each mechanism depends on a specific technical stack. At the base is an AI and machine-learning customer graph that unifies billing, device, network and support history into one predictive profile per customer, without which the data sources stay siloed and mechanisms that combine signals, such as location with billing status for a balance-aware local promotion, cannot work. Real-time charging through Online Charging Systems and Policy and Charging Rules Functions lets a promotion trigger instantly instead of in a batch cycle hours later. Complex Event Processing correlates streams of low-level signals into meaningful micro-events such as a location change, an app switch or a usage spike, and Deep Packet Inspection supplies the network-level visibility into what traffic consists of that the processing needs. Next-best-action engines paired with generative AI decide the offer and the moment and also write the campaign copy in real time, which folds creative production into the decision loop, and predictive analytics on early signs of dissatisfaction trigger retention interventions before a customer cancels. Omnichannel delivery through SMS, email, app push and call center ensures that whatever the system decides can reach the customer in the channel they are using. <span class="src">How Telcos Can Monetize Personalization with AI</span>

The two threads converge on the same structural point. Operators already hold the deep user data, real-time behavioral triggers and billing intelligence that digital platforms would covet, and PwC's five Cs show how bundling, experience, innovation and simplification can turn that position into pricing power, while the personalization proposal shows how the data itself can be monetized. The gap in both cases is not access to assets but the operating model, AI capability and architecture needed to capture value from them. <span class="src">Five commercial imperatives to help telcos regain an edge</span> <span class="src">How Telcos Can Monetize Personalization with AI</span>
