## Summary

Agentic AI shifts the bottleneck in software delivery from writing code to review, verification, release approval and decision-making, so productivity gains stall unless organizations redesign the end-to-end development system. Research from MIT, Bain and McKinsey shows that leaders realize value by codifying engineering knowledge for agents, automating verification, reorganizing into small pods with redistributed decision rights, running agent-driven workflows such as the 24-hour sprint and reinvesting freed capacity. The same shift compresses demand for traditional technology services while opening new value pools, which pushes providers toward agentic enablement, outcome-based pricing and forward-deployed delivery.

## Concept

The sources describe one pattern from several angles, which Bain calls the shifting bottleneck paradox. AI collapses the cost of writing code, so the constraint moves to review, verification, release approval and decision-making. An MIT Sloan study of more than 100,000 GitHub developers traces this through writing, committing, pull requests, merging and releasing. Cumulative coding activity rose by up to 180%, with autocomplete adding about 40%, synchronous agents 140% and asynchronous agents 180%, yet developers produced only 50% more projects and 30% more releases and app downloads and reviews did not rise, because the effect attenuates at each later human step. The study recommends smaller teams, reallocating staff to merge, release and maintenance work, and using AI to ease downstream constraints rather than accelerating a single stage. <span class="src">AI boosts worker productivity — but does that translate to final outputs?</span>

Bain's survey of 293 technology leaders finds developers completing about 21% more tasks while review time rises roughly 91%, so leaders expect a 95% productivity uplift against the 20-27% gains most capture now. Bain treats the surrounding system as the unit of optimization and names three missing parts. Engineering knowledge, usually held in senior engineers' heads, must be captured in structured specifications, conventions and escalation paths so agents inherit it once in writing, as with Stripe's agent workers that each carry a spec covering objective, scope, context, verification and constraints and together merge about 1,300 AI-authored pull requests a week under normal human review. Verification must be automated through risk scoring of each change's blast radius, policy-as-code guardrails, evaluation frameworks and audit trails, with humans reserved for exceptions, and 41% of surveyed companies expect a risk-tiered model while only 6% expect fully autonomous development. The development life cycle needs an owner, roadmap and metrics that go beyond DORA measures to separate human and agent contributions and treat risk as a scorecard dimension, and safety, risk and governance guardrails are the top enabler for scaling agents at 43%. Amazon, with up to 70% of code reviews handled by AI, and Craft Docs, which rose from 15-20 to over 100 issues a week, are cited as examples. <span class="src">The Missing Architecture for Agentic Software Development</span>

McKinsey's study of 334 product and engineering leaders shows that role redesign rather than tools separates leaders. Only 25% of directors and above report meaningful acceleration and 30% report falling productivity, with about 80% of engineers reporting average acceleration near 3% while the top 20% report 55%, a gap worth about USD 0.8 trillion. Teams that both embedded AI and redesigned roles, which McKinsey calls transformers, became top accelerators 40% of the time against 27% for adopters and 17% for experimenters. Top accelerators shrank squads from a median of about ten to seven people, split roles into definers who fuse product and design and builders who fuse development, testing and operations, wrote specifications precise enough for an agent to act on and a test suite to check, redesigned workflows around asynchronous agent night shifts, as when Sonar cut pull-request cycle time 3.4 times, gave agents scoped mandates and escalation paths, built a shared AI operations layer for identity, permissions, evaluation, logging and cost, triaged verification by risk, and ran adoption as an embedded-coaching program tracked by outcomes rather than licenses. <span class="src">Scaling the agentic product development life cycle</span>

The same study gives warning signs. Average time savings of 11.8% outrun rework reduction of 6.2%, external research cited shows 45% of AI-generated code samples failing security checks, and token, compute and AI-operations spending can reach 20% of existing labor costs. McKinsey offers progress markers such as 70% of job descriptions changed, squads of two to four people and two-day sprints. <span class="src">Scaling the agentic product development life cycle</span>

Bain's organizational study explains why roles change faster than power. Teams have shrunk to pods of three to five, AI fluency is the leading hiring criterion at 62%, three times raw coding skill, pyramid-shaped engineering organizations fell from 66% to 29%, developers expect to spend a third of their time directing agents within two years, and junior engineers fell from a third of teams to under a fifth, which removes the routine work on which judgment was once built. The skipped change is the decision layer, since gains stall when approvals, handoffs and escalations are unchanged and 70% of companies still decide centrally. Bain advises distributing decision rights and defining where humans decide, where agents act and who owns outcomes, as one AI-native company did by letting feature engineers launch their own marketing. <span class="src">The Half-Finished Redesign How AI Reshapes Software Organizations</span>

McKinsey's 24-hour sprint describes what a rewired delivery model looks like. Humans work during the day reviewing agent output, resolving ambiguity, strengthening architectural guardrails and aligning stakeholders, so their role shifts from producing artifacts to supervising and improving the system that produces them, while agents work overnight to enrich requirements, validate architecture, generate and test code and package outputs for morning review. This compresses the traditional two-week sprint, and some companies report threefold to fivefold productivity gains with 60% smaller teams. It works only on four foundations, namely a clear business vision against which agent-generated requirements can be checked, a standardized technology environment of common frameworks and modular architecture, a standard structure for the path from requirements to code so that output is predictable, and consistent engagement from the same core stakeholders across the value stream to avoid misalignment and rework. <span class="src">Rewiring software delivery for the agentic era</span>

McKinsey also argues that scaling requires extending automation beyond testing and deployment, where CI/CD can represent up to 30% of technology spend, into the larger, interpretation-heavy zone from requirements through coding, where requirements, standards and specifications sit scattered across documents and tools and humans repeatedly translate intent between artifacts. Codifying functional and nonfunctional requirements, guardrails, sequence diagrams and repositories in standardized, machine-readable formats lets the pipeline run end to end in hours with humans only at defined review gates. Agents also need organizational memory, which leaders build as knowledge graphs semantically linking customer feedback, architecture decisions, design documents, tickets, GitHub activity, incident reports and compliance rules for each domain. A librarian agent can then answer in minutes what once took weeks of interviews with several experts, every decision traces to its source, tribal knowledge becomes explicit, and ramp-up time falls, but the graph should evolve around priority domains and live programs rather than start as a top-down ontology. <span class="src">Rewiring software delivery for the agentic era</span>

Capturing the organizational payoff takes three deliberate moves. Reskilling means a central team maintains the agent factories for standardization and compliance while engineers develop judgment, code review and supervisory skills, moving from manual coordination and testing toward architecture coherence, domain modeling and AI supervision. Outer-loop roles in risk, legal, testing and procurement must be embedded from the start, for example through policy-as-code, so that they do not become an end-stage bottleneck that cancels upstream speed. Freed capacity must be reinvested to accelerate roadmaps, modernize platforms or launch products rather than absorbed without a plan. McKinsey recommends starting with the few large programs, such as legacy modernization, brownfield rebuilds and new product launches, that account for most technology spend, and expects human roles to concentrate in architecture, product judgment and system design, making institutional knowledge and technical coherence the decisive differentiators. Early implementations suggest that teams of 8-12 full-time engineers can give way to smaller pods of highly skilled professionals supervising agent-driven execution. <span class="src">Rewiring software delivery for the agentic era</span>

The same principles appear within a single discipline in QuantumBlack's design-system project. When anyone can build, explicit design intent scales judgment, so the rebuild favors foundations that AI already understands, built on shadcn and Tailwind, composition over configuration, richer structure over exhaustive documentation and room for both quick prototyping and production work, and it packages agent guidance with tokens and components so that agents inherit conventions instead of relying on handcrafted prompts, which makes AI output cheaper and more consistent. <span class="src">When everyone can build, design is the differentiator</span>

For technology services providers the same shift is a two-sided disruption. McKinsey's July 2025 survey of 200 C-suite executives at companies with revenue above USD 500 million across Asia, Europe and North America found more than 80% running agentic pilots, about 12% with scaled deployment across several functions and half planning significant scaled investment within six months, with over a third expecting AI budgets to rise more than 25% this fiscal year and nearly three-quarters expecting IT spending to grow 2-10% over two years. McKinsey nevertheless projects a 20-30% compression of the core tech services market, driven by employee productivity gains that reduce billable execution work and by large enterprises building agentic capability in-house through global capability centers that providers once only helped establish, and the work that remains shifts from execution such as monitoring and processing toward enablement such as strategy design and stakeholder engagement. The overall market still grows faster with agentic AI, at 4-7% a year to USD 1.6-1.9 trillion by 2030 against a prior forecast of 4-5%, because new value pools outweigh the legacy decline for providers positioned to capture them. <span class="src">Reengineering tech services for the agentic AI era</span>

Two pools stand out, agentic AI workflow services, covering infrastructure support, multiagent architecture design, orchestration, agent engineering, security and governance, worth nearly USD 200 billion in new enterprise spending, and business function transformation through joint human-agent operating models in knowledge roles, worth USD 100-400 billion a year. Enterprises expect as much as 15-30% of current roles' work to move to agents within three years, in technology and engineering agents, customer-facing agents, back-end function agents and vertical-specific process agents, with over 70% of the opportunity in financial services, retail, high tech, healthcare and manufacturing. Competitive boundaries blur as software vendors move toward service as software, hyperscalers offer managed agentic solutions and pure-play AI disruptors, including LLM providers building domain agents, aim to capture value directly, with one startup claiming its customer-service agents resolve up to 90% of inquiries without humans. <span class="src">Reengineering tech services for the agentic AI era</span>

McKinsey identifies four value propositions that providers may pursue differently by vertical. The agentic AI enabler provides foundational infrastructure and agent services and needs deep technical expertise and hyperscaler and LLM partnerships, the packaged agent implementer deploys prebuilt third-party agents through a partner ecosystem, the custom agent developer builds bespoke solutions needing domain expertise, and the end-to-end workflow disruptor acts as a transformational partner that redesigns entire workflows. Five capabilities distinguish leaders, namely repositioning around agentic-first opportunities rather than unscalable experimental copilots, proprietary modular architecture with built-in observability and governance, consultative domain-driven go-to-market, an operating model and talent base built around human-agent collaboration, and commercial models that tie fees to impact, since more than 70% of enterprises prefer alternatives to time-and-material billing and providers are moving toward subscription pods billed at a flat monthly fee or gain-share tied to outcomes such as cost savings. <span class="src">Reengineering tech services for the agentic AI era</span>

Bain's market-side analysis shows how sharply the sector has repriced. Tech-services share prices have fallen since early 2025, with focused digital players at about 25% of their January 2025 level, as clients demand up to 20% efficiency gains at renewal and price new work on 30-50% lower delivery costs, while measured AI productivity potential ranges 15-45% by service line. Providers are committing to productivity they have not achieved under three-to-five-year contracts, few have a repeatable commercial model and frontline teams lack confidence converting AI offerings into funded programs. Winning moves are to concentrate on one proven segment, as Coforge grew 29% on four industries and over 150 AI engagements, to position as a reinvention partner, to build a go-to-market engine that sells AI transformation, to favor depth over breadth of agent platforms, to adopt outcome-based or gain-sharing pricing, to use forward-deployed engineers as the delivery unit and to rebuild delivery rather than wrap AI around time-and-materials work. <span class="src">Tech Services Cracking the Code on AI-led Growth</span>
