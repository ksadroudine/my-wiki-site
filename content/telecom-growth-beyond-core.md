## Summary

How telecom operators are using their data advantage, trusted customer relationships, and infrastructure to expand revenue beyond core connectivity. Covers vertical-ecosystem services (B2C), ICT/cloud/cybersecurity managed services and network APIs (B2B), and private 5G networks, with real operator examples, market-sizing data, and the organizational capabilities each growth path requires.

## Concept

Telecom operators occupy a structurally advantaged position for growing revenue beyond core connectivity: their central role in the digital economy gives them real-time visibility into network usage, device behavior, location, and consumption patterns at a scale and granularity few other industries can match, and this data advantage — combined with already-trusted customer relationships and ubiquitous physical infrastructure — lets operators shift their commercial posture from selling discrete connectivity products toward delivering integrated, value-added services built around that data and trust. This shift is playing out concretely across three main directions: B2C vertical-ecosystem expansion, B2B ICT and network-API services, and private 5G networks — each already generating measurable revenue at leading operators rather than remaining purely theoretical. <span class="src">The critical bets on the future of telco value creation</span>

On the consumer side, vertical-ecosystem expansion means bundling adjacent, often third-party-sourced services — security, insurance, energy, entertainment, fintech, health — around the core connectivity relationship rather than treating connectivity as the entire product. The revenue is real: NTT Docomo already generates more than 20 percent of its revenue from beyond-core products; Spain's MasOrange has built an app-controlled electricity-and-gas service it later sold for €90 million, an insurance business (mobile, home, health, payment protection) with a cumulative target of more than 7.5 million policies and over €1.5 billion in premium volume, and a cobranded home-security business with ADT that has measurably reduced churn; Spain's Movistar gained more than 400,000 customers in the first year of a home-security joint venture with Prosegur; and Canada's Telus now derives a quarter of its revenue from B2B digital verticals spanning healthcare, agriculture, and back-office digital process solutions, a diversification strategy credited with helping the company's total shareholder return outperform the broader national telco sector. The common thread across these examples is that vertical expansion serves two goals simultaneously: it opens genuinely new revenue streams while also deepening the core connectivity relationship, since customers subscribed to multiple bundled services from the same operator are measurably "stickier" and less likely to churn than single-service customers. <span class="src">Mapping the routes to renewed success for telcos</span> <span class="src">The critical bets on the future of telco value creation</span>

On the enterprise side, B2B ICT products and services — extending from core connectivity into cloud, cybersecurity, and IT managed services — represent a growth path that can scale to as much as 20 percent of a telco's total revenue; one European telco more than doubled its cloud-related revenue and earnings within four years specifically through infrastructure upgrades, workforce upskilling, and key technology partnerships, demonstrating this isn't a marginal side business but a genuinely scalable growth line when executed with real investment behind it. Network APIs address a distinct and specifically 5G-linked opportunity: standardized, interoperable interfaces that expose network capabilities — speed on demand, low-latency connections, speed tiering, edge-compute discovery — directly to enterprise developers, letting them build applications (credit-card fraud prevention, glitch-free videoconferencing, remotely operated industrial equipment, semi-autonomous vehicles) that depend on guaranteed network performance characteristics rather than best-effort connectivity. McKinsey estimates this could unlock $100 billion to $300 billion in connectivity- and edge-computing-related revenue for telcos over five to seven years, plus a further $10 billion to $30 billion directly from the APIs themselves. A working example already generating real revenue: a joint API initiative among three Brazilian operators (TIM, Claro, Vivo), developed through the industry's GSMA Open Gateway framework, gives financial institutions continuous mobile-number verification, SIM-swap-attack detection, and instant device-location validation for fraud prevention — already reaching roughly 150 million customers, with the SIM-swap-detection API alone generating $9 million in incremental revenue. Because API value depends entirely on interoperability across networks (an API a developer can only use on one operator's footprint is far less valuable than one that works everywhere), realizing this opportunity requires an unusual degree of cross-competitor collaboration: a joint venture among Deutsche Telekom, AT&T, Orange, Singtel, Telstra, and Verizon with equipment maker Ericsson to sell interoperable network APIs globally illustrates the scale of coordination this growth path actually demands. Private 5G networks represent a third enterprise-facing path: customized, vertical-specific network architecture built for use cases like smart hospitals, connected transportation, and augmented retail experiences, already being launched by operators including T-Mobile, O2, Vodafone, and KPN and targeted specifically at logistics, manufacturing, and public-sector customers as an incremental way to monetize 5G investment beyond ordinary consumer connectivity. <span class="src">Mapping the routes to renewed success for telcos</span> <span class="src">The critical bets on the future of telco value creation</span>

A further, less immediately obvious growth avenue is sustainability and green energy: telcos' combination of core connectivity assets, proprietary consumer data, extensive retail distribution footprint, field technician workforce, and existing in-home customer relationships positions them credibly to offer 5G-enabled sustainability applications, net-zero home solutions, and green cloud services. This taps into both a large external opportunity ($9 trillion to $12 trillion of value expected from the broader net-zero economic transition) and documented consumer willingness to pay a 15 to 30 percent premium for sustainably produced goods and services, alongside young talent increasingly favoring employers with genuine, demonstrated ESG commitments — meaning this growth path serves talent-attraction and investor-relations goals simultaneously with direct revenue generation. <span class="src">Mapping the routes to renewed success for telcos</span>

Achieving genuine commercial excellence across these growth paths — rather than a scattering of underperforming side bets, the historical pattern the source notes telcos have struggled with — requires specific enabling capabilities. Operators need to embrace a fundamentally different kind of partner ecosystem, striking deals directly with vertical-market leaders (insurers, security firms, energy companies) rather than treating partnerships purely as technology vendor relationships, alongside an internal integration platform that makes bundling, activation, and ongoing management of these non-core services operationally straightforward rather than a bespoke project each time. For ICT-driven B2B growth specifically, operators need the technical foundation to support hyperscaler and cybersecurity/software alliances — a modular platform capable of managing full-stack solutions, plus a specialized enterprise salesforce equipped to serve demanding business customers with the integrated delivery and support they expect. For network APIs to scale, the industry needs common application-layer standards that minimize friction for the developers actually building on top of them, and deliberate integration of those APIs with hyperscaler platforms to extend their practical reach. And for private 5G specifically, operators need reliable, enterprise-grade wireless spectrum access to back the service guarantees such networks require, frequently developed in partnership with industrial players or hyperscalers who contribute complementary hardware, software, and services tailored to the specific vertical being served. <span class="src">The critical bets on the future of telco value creation</span>

<span class="src">B2C growth beyond the core</span> (McKinsey, February 2026) supplies the macro rationale and quantified scale behind the B2C vertical-ecosystem expansion this concept's other sources case-study individually. It opens by diagnosing why traditional B2C telco growth has stalled: unlimited voice and data plans have exhausted upsell opportunities, fiber uptake is commoditizing what used to be a premium gigabit product, fixed-mobile convergence has already reached 50%-plus penetration in major markets like Belgium, Portugal, and Spain (accompanied by significant linear-TV decline), and headline price increases retain less than half their value once customers migrate to cheaper front-book offers. Against this backdrop, the brief sizes the beyond-the-core opportunity at a scale well beyond incremental adjacency: by 2035 the combined B2B and B2C integrated network economy is projected at roughly $125 trillion globally, with specific B2C-relevant adjacent domains — housing (~$7.4 trillion), health (~$7.9 trillion), digital content (~$4.8 trillion), mobility (~$3.0 trillion), education (~$5.5 trillion), and B2C marketplaces (~$12.1 trillion) — offering meaningful headroom, further reinforced by roughly 70 percent of consumers already saying they're willing to buy at least one adjacent service through their telco. The financial profile is structurally attractive too: distributor and B2B2C partnership models require limited incremental capital, leverage assets telcos already have (retail, billing, customer care, field force), and generate high-margin revenue while reinforcing the core connectivity business through higher engagement and reduced churn — a pattern investors are increasingly rewarding with higher valuation multiples for integrated telcos relative to pure connectivity plays. While earlier waves of beyond-the-core experimentation frequently failed or stayed unprofitable, the brief reports that leading operators today derive between 5 and 40 percent of total revenue (roughly €1 billion to €15 billion annually) from adjacencies including fintech, insurance, digital media, energy, and security, demonstrating the model scales when pursued with focus and the right operating model. The brief's central diagnostic claim is that the primary remaining barrier is organizational and operational rather than strategic: telco structures built for scale, reliability, and cost efficiency generally lack the speed, experimentation, and customer-centricity that ecosystem plays demand. Success requires a platform-led approach with clear organizational focus — an operating model with real structure, process, people, and technology investment, P&L accountability linked to the core business, and shared targets and incentives — and, once early results validate a vertical, "bringing it together" around an overarching customer value proposition with a platform-first design centered on orchestration, integration, and bundling rather than treating each new offering as a standalone product. <span class="src">B2C growth beyond the core</span>
