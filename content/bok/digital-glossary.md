## Summary

This glossary defines the terms that appear most often in the Digital Business and Platforms part of the Book of Knowledge. Each entry gives a short plain-language definition and points to the Concept where the term is explained in depth. Terms are listed in alphabetical order.

## Glossary of Digital Business Terms

| Term | Meaning | See |
|------|---------|-----|
| API (application programming interface) | A contract in which a provider commits to certain functions, to notice of changes and often to usage limits, so that developers can build applications on it. | [API Strategy and Digital Value Chain Unbundling](#/concept/api-and-digital-value-chain-strategy) |
| Bridger | A person who builds partnerships across organizational boundaries by translating between different priorities, limits and working styles. | [Digital Transformation Leadership](#/concept/digital-transformation) |
| Cold start | The problem a new platform faces when it needs both producers and consumers before either side sees much value. | [Platform Business Models and Network Effects](#/concept/platform-business-models) |
| Core interaction | The single most important recurring exchange of value that draws users to a platform, made of participants, a value unit and a filter. | [Platform Business Models and Network Effects](#/concept/platform-business-models) |
| Cross-side network effect | The effect that users on one side of a platform have on the value for users on the other side. | [Platform Business Models and Network Effects](#/concept/platform-business-models) |
| Data-informed | Using data to complement human judgment, in contrast with data-driven, which employees can read as replacing their judgment. | [Digital Transformation Leadership](#/concept/digital-transformation) |
| Differential pricing | Charging different prices to different customer groups for essentially the same good. | [Information Goods Economics](#/concept/information-goods-economics) |
| Digital dexterity | The state of a workforce that is both willing and able to use new technologies such as generative AI. | [Digital Transformation Leadership](#/concept/digital-transformation) |
| Disintermediation | What happens when participants, once connected by a platform, deal directly with each other and bypass it. | [Platform Business Models and Network Effects](#/concept/platform-business-models) |
| Experience good | A good whose value a buyer cannot judge without consuming it. Information is one every time it is consumed. | [Information Goods Economics](#/concept/information-goods-economics) |
| Filter | The algorithmic mechanism that decides which value units reach which consumers on a platform. | [Platform Business Models and Network Effects](#/concept/platform-business-models) |
| First-copy cost | The high fixed cost of creating an information good, after which each further copy costs almost nothing. | [Information Goods Economics](#/concept/information-goods-economics) |
| Hyperspecialist | A firm that performs one narrow task at scale for many clients, which a client embeds in its workflow through APIs. | [API Strategy and Digital Value Chain Unbundling](#/concept/api-and-digital-value-chain-strategy) |
| Keystone strategy | A strategy in which the hub of an ecosystem aligns its incentives with the long-term health of the ecosystem it depends on. | [Platform Business Models and Network Effects](#/concept/platform-business-models) |
| Learning effect | The gain in value that comes when more data flowing through a network improves its algorithms. | [Platform Business Models and Network Effects](#/concept/platform-business-models) |
| Limit pricing | Pricing low enough to make entry unattractive to a would-be competitor. | [Information Goods Economics](#/concept/information-goods-economics) |
| Lock-in | A situation in which switching costs keep a customer with a supplier, common where systems are built from complementary components. | [Information Goods Economics](#/concept/information-goods-economics) |
| Multihoming | The use of several competing platforms at the same time by users or providers. | [Platform Business Models and Network Effects](#/concept/platform-business-models) |
| Network bridging | Connecting a firm's network to a previously separate network, as Uber did when it entered food delivery. | [Platform Business Models and Network Effects](#/concept/platform-business-models) |
| Network effect | The rise in a product's value to each user as more users join. | [Information Goods Economics](#/concept/information-goods-economics) |
| Orchestrator | A firm that coordinates an end-to-end workflow and acts as the customer's contact without controlling every underlying capability. | [API Strategy and Digital Value Chain Unbundling](#/concept/api-and-digital-value-chain-strategy) |
| Pipeline business | A business that designs, builds and pushes a product through a linear chain to a consumer. | [Platform Business Models and Network Effects](#/concept/platform-business-models) |
| Platform | Infrastructure that lets outside producers and consumers find each other, transact and exchange value. | [Platform Business Models and Network Effects](#/concept/platform-business-models) |
| Value unit | The information, content or service that is exchanged on a platform, which the platform usually does not create or control. | [Platform Business Models and Network Effects](#/concept/platform-business-models) |
| VRIN | A test of strategic assets, which must be valuable, rare, inimitable and nonsubstitutable. | [Digital Transformation Leadership](#/concept/digital-transformation) |
| Zero-click commerce | Buying in which an AI agent searches, chooses and purchases without the user visiting a platform interface where advertising could intervene. | [Platform Business Models and Network Effects](#/concept/platform-business-models) |
