## Summary

Many analytics and AI models never reach production, and a new role, the data product manager, exists to close that gap. This chapter explains what that role does, why the Chief Data Officer role is unstable and why data leadership may dissolve into broader business roles. It also covers how data science is changing from a craft into an industrial discipline, when a dedicated Chief AI Officer helps and when it hurts, and why some organizations combine technology oversight with change management in one transformation role.

## Key Ideas

- Most data scientists see 20 percent or fewer of their models reach production, because the work is usually considered finished when the model fits the data and not when people adopt it.
- A data product manager translates business needs into data products that people use, and the job depends more on coordination than on deep technical skill.
- The Chief Data Officer role is under pressure to show profit, from decentralizing business units and from generative AI, and many holders stay less than three years.
- A Chief AI Officer adds the most value in the middle stages of AI maturity and should be handed off once AI is part of normal operations.
- AI implementation is mostly an organizational challenge, which is why some companies create a single role that combines technology oversight with change management.

## Data Leadership and Roles

### The production gap and the data product manager

A survey of data scientists found that the majority saw 20 percent or fewer of their models reach production. The cause is that data scientists usually consider their job done once a model fits the data well, and not once it is adopted and delivering lasting value. HBR argues that a new role exists to close this gap, the data product manager. This person turns business needs into data products, which are analytics and AI models packaged so that people actually use them.

This role needs coordination skill more than deep technical expertise. A data product manager leads a diverse team and talks with the business leaders whose operations the model will change. The manager understands how data is captured, how good it is and how it is integrated, and understands basic AI and machine-learning ideas, including model drift, which is the decline in accuracy as the world changes. The manager is not expected to build the model or to redesign the business processes.

The results can be large. At Vista, data products generated an incremental $90 million in profit. At Regions Bank, they have earned or saved hundreds of millions of dollars. Both companies developed products through a cycle that starts with a minimum viable product, which is a bare-bones first version. Vista's chief data officer prefers a minimum lovable product, because customers responded poorly to products that felt unpolished. Regions Bank created a distinct role, the data product partner, who sits inside a part of the bank and attends its staff meetings. This person translates in both directions between business needs and technical solutions. Most of these partners came from the business side with a technical or analytical orientation.

### The unstable Chief Data Officer

Investment in data and AI is growing. Overall, 98.4 percent of organizations are increasing it, up from 82.2 percent a year earlier, and 84.3 percent have appointed a Chief Data Officer or Chief Data and Analytics Officer, up from 12 percent in 2012. Yet only 47.6 percent describe the role as very successful and well established. More than half of these executives, 53.7 percent, hold the job for less than three years, and 24.1 percent for less than two.

Three forces are destabilizing the role.

- **Pressure to create value.** Chief data officers are increasingly judged by their contribution to profit, and not exempted as a novel, unproven function. One retail chief financial officer said that all the company had to show for its data investments was prettier dashboards.
- **Decentralization.** As an organization's data maturity rises, business units take ownership of their own data assets, talent and budgets, which shrinks the central mandate.
- **Generative AI.** Many chief data officers did not engage enough with AI's business potential, and 33.1 percent of surveyed organizations appointed a separate AI leader, often the chief technology officer or chief financial officer.

MIT Sloan Management Review (MIT SMR) does not propose a better-defended mandate. It proposes that data leadership dissolve into broader business leadership, in three possible directions.

- A **commercial or operational leader**, such as the chief operating officer or chief commercial officer, who already carries profit accountability that the average chief data officer struggles to claim.
- A **technology or product leader**, such as the chief technology officer or chief product officer, who already needs enterprise architecture and the skill of turning customer signals into product decisions.
- A **governance leader**, such as the chief financial officer or chief legal officer, who already owns audit, risk and information-asset accountability at board level.

Executives themselves expect the role to last. In the survey, 70.8 percent believe the Chief Data Officer will become a permanent member of the senior team. MIT SMR's authors read that result skeptically. It may show a belief that accountability for data belongs at the top, and not evidence that the standalone title will last. Already 36.3 percent of chief data officers report to a business leader, such as the chief executive, the president or the chief operating officer, and not to technology leadership. Named examples of data leadership merging into a broader technology role include executives at TIAA, First Group and Travelers, who report directly to their chief executives. ### Data science becomes an industrial discipline

A 2024 trends survey found that data science is moving away from an artisanal craft and toward a discipline built on tools and platforms. These include systems for running machine-learning operations, shared stores of model inputs and automated retraining. The data scientist role is splitting into a wider cast of specialists, such as data engineers, machine learning engineers, business translators and data product managers. A parallel rise in citizen data science means that quantitatively literate business people can build their own models with automated machine-learning tools and early language-model analysis tools.

The same survey found 87 percent of respondents confused about where to turn for data- and technology-oriented services, and 79 percent said that a lack of collaboration among technology leaders had hindered their organization. This was early evidence of the dissolution later described by MIT SMR.

### When a Chief AI Officer helps

Whether to appoint a Chief AI Officer should depend on an organization's AI maturity, not on enthusiasm. The role adds the most value in the middle stages, first by consolidating fragmented pilot projects and then by guiding selective scale-up. It adds little at the earliest stage, when there is no AI activity to coordinate. It becomes counterproductive once AI is fully integrated into normal operations, and at that point the role should be handed off and not made permanent.

The downsides mirror the experience with the Chief Data Officer. There can be turf conflict with the chief information, technology and operating officers. There is a structural incentive to favor AI solutions over simpler fixes that need no AI. And there is added cost that a weak business case cannot justify. One data office grew to more than 100 full-time staff before a cost-cutting review reversed course.

### Technology plus change management in one role

A proposed alternative is the chief innovation and transformation officer. It combines oversight of AI technology with responsibility for organizational change, on the argument that AI implementation is mostly an organizational challenge. Chief information officers and chief technology officers usually have neither the mandate nor the time to manage AI's human and cultural effects. Two failures show what can go wrong when nobody does. Zillow lost $300 million on a home-buying program based on AI valuations. Air Canada's chatbot gave incorrect bereavement-fare guidance to grieving customers.

Real organizations already fill roles of this shape under inconsistent titles. PepsiCo has a chief strategy and transformation officer. Standard Chartered has a chief transformation, technology and operations officer. TIAA has a chief operating, information and digital officer. Each combines technical and organizational-change accountability in one seat. Boston Consulting Group found that hires of chief transformation officers rose more than 140 percent from 2019 to 2021, and that companies making such hires saw a significant increase in total shareholder return the following year. This suggests that the consolidation is a rewarded response and not only a proposal.
