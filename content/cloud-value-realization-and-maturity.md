## Summary

Most cloud transformations capture only a fraction of their potential value, typically plateauing at about 35 percent after five years, and the gap has less to do with technology than with ambition, business engagement and execution capability. Research from Accenture, Bain, BCG, McKinsey, Forrester and PwC converges on the finding that value comes from two sources, bottom-line efficiency and top-line innovation, and that companies in the highest maturity states treat cloud as a company-wide effort owned jointly by business and IT. The concept covers why programs stall, the warning signs and turnaround interventions for struggling ones, maturity models that describe what leaders do differently in cloud and now AI, and the actions that move an organization up the curve.

## Concept

The research starts from a shared observation: adopting the cloud is no longer the hard part for most large organizations, and capturing the value it makes possible is. Accenture's survey of 750 executives found that only 37 percent of C-level leaders said they had fully achieved expected cloud outcomes, barely up from 35 percent two years earlier, even though the pandemic compressed what companies had planned as an eight-to-ten-year move from roughly 20-30 percent of work in the cloud toward about 80 percent into roughly three years. Bain quantifies the shortfall, finding that only about one company in twenty sets its ambition high enough to target the full potential and that most plateau at roughly 35 percent of it after about five years, once the euphoria of a few successful pilots fades. BCG adds that more than half of cloud transformations fail to achieve their intended benefits within three years, which represents hundreds of millions of dollars in losses for a typical large company, and that a transformation that has gone off track is very hard to turn around. McKinsey sizes the prize at more than $1 trillion of run-rate EBITDA across Fortune 500 companies by 2030, with value concentrated in high tech, oil and gas, retail, healthcare, insurance and banking and with almost every industry showing potential for a rise of 20 percent or more. <span class="src">What CEOs Need to Know About the Cloud in 2021</span> <span class="src">Achieving Maximum Potential From a Cloud Transformation</span> <span class="src">Is Your Cloud Transformation Struggling Here’s How to Fix It.</span> <span class="src">Cloud’s trillion-dollar prize is up for grabs</span>

Value comes from two sources that companies pursue unevenly. Bottom-line value from higher developer productivity and lower unit costs, such as roughly a fifth off compute and storage unit costs and software development time cut by half or more, is what most firms target first and almost exclusively. Top-line value from cloud as an innovation platform, for example launching products months ahead of competitors or responding quickly to disruptions, as The Wall Street Journal did when it used AWS machine-learning services to build a searchable candidate-quotes database in four months ahead of the 2020 US election, is under-captured despite being potentially the larger prize. The stakes are structural, because a cloud-enabled insurgent can hold a 60 to 70 percent operating-cost advantage plus faster time to market over an incumbent weighed down by legacy technology, which exposes incumbents in banking, insurance, consumer products, pharmaceuticals and utilities to being outhustled. McKinsey's three dimensions describe increasing ambition. Rejuvenate uses cloud to cut cost and risk in legacy IT and core operations, and effective usage improves application development and maintenance productivity by 38 percent and infrastructure cost efficiency by 29 percent for migrated applications and could cut downtime about 57 percent against projected 2030 losses of $650 billion from downtime and breaches. Innovate uses on-demand infrastructure and cloud-native tooling (containers, microservices, DevOps, CI/CD, serverless) to enable new use cases, faster product development and rapid scaling into new segments and geographies. Pioneer extends cloud toward emerging technologies such as quantum computing, blockchain, augmented and virtual reality and 3D printing once cloud maturity is high, an area McKinsey could not yet quantify. <span class="src">Achieving Maximum Potential From a Cloud Transformation</span> <span class="src">Cloud’s trillion-dollar prize is up for grabs</span>

Several barriers explain why programs stall. Bain's three recurring barriers are the shortage of cloud engineering talent, the difficulty of working within legacy environments and cultural habits that keep teams working the old way. The decisive factor in breaking through the 35 percent ceiling is whether the business side, and not only technology, is bought into cloud as an innovation platform, since initiatives led by IT with little business or finance engagement lose momentum, while those built on a business case that includes innovation upside such as faster time to market and new revenue earn whole-company support. Accenture identifies five recurring CEO-level concerns. On data trust, public providers generally run more comprehensive security programs than most companies can on premises, but genuinely sensitive consumer or healthcare data often still warrants a hybrid model. On legacy infrastructure, 70 percent of C-level leaders in a 2018 survey wanted to keep legacy systems running as long as possible, which makes a lift-and-shift first step a pragmatic start rather than an ideal end state. On choosing a service model, SaaS enabled Rolls-Royce to deploy a cloud HR system in 46 countries overnight and IaaS and PaaS let Del Monte transform core IT infrastructure in under four months. On business reimagination, Takeda uses edge computing so hemophilia patients can monitor enzyme levels at home, ENGIE unified customer data across 70 countries and 24 business units, and Barcelona manages transport, waste, water and energy on one cloud platform, and 87 percent of surveyed leaders said cloud would be critical or moderately important to their sustainability goals. On skills, nearly half of CEOs at low-adoption companies and three in ten at high-adoption companies (75 percent or more of work in the cloud) cite shortages, so skills investment does not taper once migration succeeds, as Takeda's creation of hundreds of specialized roles and upskilling of thousands of employees illustrates. <span class="src">Achieving Maximum Potential From a Cloud Transformation</span> <span class="src">What CEOs Need to Know About the Cloud in 2021</span>

BCG's six warning signs show how an off-track transformation looks from the inside, and the risk compounds as more appear together. The absence of clear goals, agreed metrics or milestones, or early missed milestones, signals poor stakeholder alignment or weak execution rigor. Migration sequencing that is not centrally managed lets IT teams scope and sequence their own moves without assessing strategic importance or technical complexity, which produces rework and overruns. Weak cost controls show when migrated-application costs rise faster than inflation. Undefined minimum cloud skills or no coordinated training leave application teams unprepared for the operational responsibilities migration hands them. Technical standards left unenforced, often deliberately to smooth team buy-in, produce expensive unnecessary complexity. Applications migrated mainly to infrastructure as a service forfeit the benefits of flexible scaling, and BCG finds cloud transitions run as part of a broader business transformation generate 12 to 15 times the benefits of purely infrastructure-focused ones. The six turnaround interventions address these causes directly. A control tower defines intended benefits and communicates them to application teams along with technical standards across nine topic areas, which few companies define systematically. Redefined development and operations roles reassign the duties that shift to application teams and turn infrastructure operations into cloud platform teams offering on-demand self-service. A formal minimum-skills definition and upskilling program with standard curricula per role, including for vendor-supplied staff, closes the training gap. A technical accelerator program places outside migration specialists alongside inexperienced teams, a dedicated cloud FinOps capability makes cost drivers visible through automated dashboards and benchmarking, and automation enforces policies continuously instead of through periodic manual review. One client whose transformation had stalled for four years, with slightly more than half its applications in the cloud and digital delivery slowing, used this approach to complete the migration backlog in 18 months, cut run costs on previously migrated applications by more than 10 percent and raise its average application security score from below the required level to roughly 80 percent. <span class="src">Is Your Cloud Transformation Struggling Here’s How to Fix It.</span>

Bain's playbook for breaking through the plateau has six elements. Define cloud efforts customer-back, so executives can trace investment to customer outcomes such as cross-selling and loyalty. Build a full-potential business case that captures every source of value, namely unit cost, developer productivity, defect reduction, new revenue and customer loyalty, instead of cost savings alone. Design the strategy to include new ways of working that accelerate operating-model change rather than only moving infrastructure. Invest in stages, building testing environments, product architectures and migration plans deliberately. Educate executives and frontline staff continuously so the organization keeps the most expansive view of cloud's benefits instead of narrowing back to cost thinking. Invest in engineering talent and culture by combining reskilling with hiring. The overarching discipline is to aim high from the outset and sustain momentum past the point where initial enthusiasm fades. McKinsey's four actions are consistent with this. Set an ambitious, urgent business aspiration rather than a conservative target. Pursue a hard-headed economic case covering both savings and business acceleration, adjusted for transformation risk and sequenced by business domain. Adopt agile, cloud-native ways of working through a DevSecFinOps approach in which small cross-functional teams deliver business cases rather than applications in rapid iterations, with security embedded and end-to-end automation built in. Build a standardized, automated platform that gives developers, cloud's primary daily consumers, a strong self-service experience through API-based provisioning. <span class="src">Achieving Maximum Potential From a Cloud Transformation</span> <span class="src">Cloud’s trillion-dollar prize is up for grabs</span>

Bain also turns the value potential into a maturity model describing where companies sit, with most clustered in the first two states. Avoiders still operate entirely on their own data centers, often citing regulatory requirements or risk and underestimating cloud, and they miss faster innovation and cloud-enabled profit pools that accrue to early movers. Migrators, the largest group, have delegated cloud to IT without a comprehensive value-based strategy and pursue it from a narrow infrastructure lens. About 60 percent of them lift and shift without reengineering applications, often expecting savings that may never appear and sometimes paying for cloud usage and a lingering on-premises footprint at once, while the other 40 percent modernize as they migrate. Even these companies report that roughly 40 percent of their cloud execution capabilities are insufficient, which produces delayed programs, underinvestment and shadow IT, and it can trap organizations in a cycle in which business leaders, who have only seen cost overruns and delay, disengage and make it harder for IT to reopen the strategic conversation. Companies at this stage often lose control of total cost of ownership, yet deliberate cost and value optimization can improve it by 20 to 30 percent. Explorers break from the cycle through individual leaders, often a CIO or CDO paired with a business leader, who understand cloud's value but produce isolated successes through trial and error rather than a repeatable engine. Strategic Value Creators treat cloud as a company-wide effort aimed at new revenue streams, customer-facing services and broad technology adoption, and are the rarest state. Moderna started building strategic cloud capability at its 2010 founding, when its clinical-trials team and IT jointly built a digital lab, so its accelerated vaccine timeline, with the first clinical batch of its COVID-19 candidate delivered 42 days after viral sequencing and its digital manufacturing model copied onto partner facilities, reflected a decade of deliberate investment. Capital One, the first bank to announce an all-in cloud commitment in 2015, has used cloud-enabled AI for real-time fraud detection, speech-recognition customer experience, automated call-center validation and real-time availability of siloed data. Bain identifies three shared success factors, namely genuine business engagement from top leadership, deep collaboration between business and technology aimed at accelerating existing initiatives and finding new high-value ones, and real investment in execution capability behind the ambition. <span class="src">Harnessing the Value of Cloud</span> <span class="src">What CEOs Need to Know About the Cloud in 2021</span>

PwC extends the same leaders-versus-laggards framing into the AI era. Its 2024 survey of 1,030 US executives, from companies with at least $500 million in revenue, identified 124 Top Performers, 12 percent of the sample, which scored at the top of both a cloud-maturity index and a generative AI maturity index and were roughly twice as likely as others to have achieved measurable value in each of ten value categories. Four behaviors distinguish them. First, they treat AI-powered cloud architecture as integrated business strategy, with 67 percent holding a formal organization-wide AI strategy against 37 percent of others and 72 percent reporting all-in cloud adoption for data modernization against 33 percent, the widest gap in the survey. Second, they modernize data specifically for generative AI, on the logic that as models become commodities an organization's proprietary data and knowledge become the differentiator, and 98 percent of Top Performers say their data architecture and governance are well equipped for generative AI, 69 percent have implemented generative-AI-oriented data modernization against 31 percent of others, and larger shares of their front-, middle- and back-office data live in the cloud (82, 74 and 79 percent against 63, 52 and 61 percent). Third, they evolve IT itself with AI-powered and agentic workflows, with 65 percent adopting AI holistically across application development and management against 28 percent of others, an average 23-point gap across 15 IT categories and efficiency gains above 30 percent in software development. Fourth, and least mature even among them, they reset cloud provider relationships, rating providers more favorably on performance transparency, support and engagement, yet only 35 percent actively renegotiate pricing, contracts or service levels (38 percent of others do) despite AI-driven service-delivery efficiencies of 20 to 40 percent. <span class="src">2024 Cloud and AI Business Survey</span>

Forrester addresses organizations more than a decade into cloud adoption. Leaders are no longer dipping a toe, as examples such as the US Air Force's Platform One, Volvo's autonomous-vehicle development, BP's sustainability work and Lowe's pandemic e-commerce scaling show, and each organization needs its own North Star of customized guiding principles, since good enough can beat best in class if it costs less and meets the need. It also needs a modern operating model spanning customers, value, capabilities, structure, governance and leadership, a documented strategy and roadmap, a governance and resilience plan and a tech stack revisited on a frequent formal cadence. On this base Forrester's next-generation model groups seven principles under each of three headings. For platforms, leaders push for intelligence and automation using AIOps and observability, abstract rather than eliminate complexity through integrated delivery platforms, compose experiences increasingly through Kubernetes (seamless composition remaining a work in progress), build dedicated platform teams (53 percent of enterprise cloud decision-makers in its 2023 survey report this structure), diversify risk through deliberate multicloud strategy, pursue workload affinity, the still-emerging vision of applications that move between platforms without rework through Kubernetes and WebAssembly, and use modern cloud-native architectures. For partners, leaders expect systems integrators and vendors to be powered by automation and analytics, platform-based, advisory-led from the start of a journey, responsive and flexible, transformative in a continuous rather than episodic sense, cocreative with dedicated joint teams and oriented to outcomes and experience, increasingly through outcome-based contracts and experience-based license agreements. For practices, leaders organize around persistent product-led teams, treat agile and DevOps as one delivery process, replace traditional IT administration with site reliability and platform engineering, extend FinOps into broader IT financial management, build Zero Trust and application security on providers' own foundations, cultivate creative resilience through chaos engineering and blameless retrospectives and use policy as code to catch compliance issues early. <span class="src">Your Next-Generation Cloud Strategy Model</span>

Two further principles temper the model. Cloud is not always the right answer, because value already invested in a data center accrues over time and starting over carries an easily underestimated opportunity cost, so most enterprise strategies should deliberately integrate cloud with mainframes, edge computing or application-specific hardware, as one large software company's IT director did by keeping strategic intellectual property on a mainframe with modern DevOps practices layered on top while replacing everything else with SaaS. And added complexity must return added business value, because maximally flexible, heavily customized architecture can slow a team without commensurate benefit, so leaders should prioritize speed to market, customer experience and reduced capital cost over architecturally perfect but slow decisions and choose deliberately where to enforce standardization and where to allow flexibility. Forrester's 2023 Infrastructure Cloud Survey found the average enterprise spent $35 million on public cloud in the prior 12 months, 14 percent of its IT budget, which it argues demands the diligence of any major capital decision. <span class="src">Modernize With Cloud And New Computing Architectures</span>

Taken together, the sources agree that the gap between leaders and laggards is explained by ambition, joint ownership by business and technology, operating-model change and sustained investment in skills, not by technology sophistication. They differ in emphasis, with Bain focused on aiming high from the outset, BCG on recognizing and recovering from trouble, McKinsey on sizing the value and the actions to capture it, Forrester on the shape of a mature strategy and PwC on how data and AI now widen the gap, and each offers a different lens on the same transformation. <span class="src">Achieving Maximum Potential From a Cloud Transformation</span> <span class="src">Is Your Cloud Transformation Struggling Here’s How to Fix It.</span> <span class="src">Cloud’s trillion-dollar prize is up for grabs</span> <span class="src">Your Next-Generation Cloud Strategy Model</span> <span class="src">2024 Cloud and AI Business Survey</span>

## Open Questions

- The sources do not reconcile how one organization should sequence these frameworks in practice, for example whether Bain's advice to aim high from the start and BCG's early-warning diagnostic apply to the same transformation at once or to different points in its life cycle.
- None of the sources says whether the maturity gap between leaders and laggards is closing or widening, meaning whether laggards are catching up to where leaders were a few years earlier or leaders are pulling further ahead as the technology advances.
