## Summary

Predictive AI and generative AI are transforming two distinct layers of the sales function — predictive AI optimizes internal sales operations like territory design, quotas, and forecasting, while generative AI helps individual reps conduct sharper customer-discovery research before every conversation. Both shift sales from generic, volume-driven outreach toward customer-specific, insight-driven engagement. Two 2026 consulting studies add evidence on agentic AI at the top of the funnel and a five-journey model for rewiring B2B commercial operations.

## Highlights

- A 2023 SBI survey found 94% of executives want to integrate AI into their sales programs, but 87% have neither attempted it nor understand how to proceed — a striking gap between desire and execution that mirrors McKinsey's finding that only 15% of business machine-learning projects succeed overall.
- Predictive AI-driven sales performance management (SPM) realigns territories, quotas, and incentives using precision forecasting, addressing the common failure pattern of overcrowded sales territories (too many reps competing for too few accounts) and misaligned quotas that drive top performers to leave.
- A composite medical-device company case study (NovaMed) used AI-driven SPM to reverse three consecutive years of revenue decline within two years, applying five drivers: forecasting accuracy, account scoring (prioritizing high-potential accounts instead of chasing every deal), territory and quota optimization, seller capacity planning, and cross-functional alignment across sales, marketing, and customer success.
- B2B buyer willingness to engage with sales teams is declining structurally — McKinsey found the share of B2B buyers wanting in-person interactions with new suppliers fell from 50% to 35% over five years, while Gartner found 64% of B2B buyers feel overwhelmed by the volume of sales and marketing communications they receive.
- The response to declining buyer access should be improving outreach quality, not increasing volume: a five-step, AI-assisted discovery process (understand the client's challenges and goals, identify the metrics tied to those challenges, find the stakeholder who owns those metrics, assess product fit, then open with concise relevant messaging) let two sales reps close six-figure deals using under two hours of AI-assisted research each.
- One rep used a single prompt asking an LLM to analyze a prospect's annual reports for financial KPI trends, surfacing that the prospect's cost of goods sold was rising and 10 percentage points higher than its nearest competitor — intelligence that previously would have required deep discovery conversations or extensive manual research to uncover.
- A second rep used a prompt asking an LLM to identify recurring analyst questions across three quarters of earnings-call transcripts, surfacing that a strategic account's leadership had made little visible progress on a customer-concentration risk repeatedly flagged by investment analysts — intelligence that reframed a stalled account review into a top-to-top strategic meeting and a sole-source contract renewal.
- Both predictive and generative AI applications in sales face the same underlying organizational obstacle: sales leaders who view sales as fundamentally relationship-driven rather than data-driven resist the cultural shift required, meaning successful adoption depends as much on training, clear communication that AI is meant to augment rather than replace sales judgment, and organizational buy-in as on the technology itself.

- Wins concentrate at the top of the funnel (lead identification, qualification and prioritisation), while mid-funnel handoffs between AI agents and reps are the hardest part and depend on mature data architecture, centralised governance and a clear playbook; 37% named enhanced decision-making as the single most effective change. <span class="src">4 key insights that show agentic AI is winning in sales</span>
- McKinsey's 2026 B2B Pulse Survey (nearly 4,000 buyers and sellers in 13 countries) finds high-growth firms are three times as likely to have raised AI investment by double digits (71% versus 25%), and that fewer than 10% of organisations have scaled AI in any given function. <span class="src">The future of B2B sales How growth champions rewire their playbooks with AI</span>

## Concept

<span class="src">Five Ways Predictive AI Can Improve Sales Performance Management</span> (MIT SMR, November 2024) addresses the internal, operational layer of the sales function: how predictive AI can optimize sales performance management (SPM) — the strategic alignment of territories, quotas, and incentives. Despite the SBI survey finding a wide gap between executive enthusiasm (94%) and actual implementation experience (87% having neither attempted nor understood how to proceed), the article argues the technology's impact is proven through a detailed composite case study, NovaMed, a medical-device company whose outdated sales-territory structure and misaligned incentives had driven three years of revenue decline and top-performer attrition before implementing AI-driven SPM. The article specifies five drivers of value: forecasting accuracy (using historical and market data to anticipate demand and customer spend rather than reactive, instinct-based planning); account scoring (prioritizing high-potential accounts using behavioral data rather than the common mistake of chasing every possible deal); territory and quota optimization (setting quotas at the motivational "sweet spot" between too easy and too challenging, and distributing accounts fairly across a sales team); seller capacity planning (right-sizing the sales force for each market segment's complexity); and cross-functional alignment (breaking down silos between sales, marketing, and customer success by sharing data and goals across the full "revenue engine," not just the sales function narrowly). The article closes by identifying the primary obstacle to realizing this value as cultural rather than technical: sales leaders who conceive of selling as fundamentally relationship-driven, not data-driven, resist the organizational reshuffling and incentive redesign AI-driven SPM actually requires.

<span class="src">How Sales Teams Can Use Gen AI to Discover What Clients Need</span> (HBR, February 2025) addresses the external, individual-rep layer of the same function: how generative AI can sharpen the customer-discovery research that precedes any sales conversation. Motivated by the same structural pressure the SPM article's account-scoring driver responds to — B2B buyers are increasingly unwilling to engage with sales outreach, with McKinsey finding in-person interaction willingness has fallen from 50 to 35 percent over five years and Gartner finding 64 percent of B2B buyers feel overwhelmed by sales communications volume — the article argues the correct response is improving outreach quality rather than increasing volume. Two case examples illustrate a five-step, AI-assisted process: a technology-company rep used a single prompt asking an LLM to analyze a mining-company prospect's annual reports for financial KPI trends, surfacing a rising, above-peer cost-of-goods-sold problem that let her craft a targeted 200-word LinkedIn message to a newly hired supply-chain VP, closing a $250,000 deal within two hours of total AI-assisted research; a metals-industry account manager facing a stalled strategic account used a prompt analyzing three quarters of earnings-call transcripts for recurring analyst concerns, surfacing an unresolved customer-concentration risk that reframed a routine business review into a top-to-top strategic meeting and secured a sole-source contract renewal. The article distills the underlying method into five steps — understand the client's major challenges and goals, identify the metrics tied to those challenges, find the specific stakeholder who owns those metrics, assess how the seller's product improves those metrics, and open with concise, relevant messaging tying it all together — noting AI proved most valuable specifically in the challenge-understanding, metric-identification, and message-crafting steps.

Read together, the two articles describe AI transforming sales at two different organizational altitudes that reinforce each other: the SPM article optimizes how a sales organization allocates its people, territories, and incentives to focus effort on the right accounts in the first place, while the discovery article optimizes how an individual rep engages once pointed at a given account — both are responses to the same underlying market pressure (declining buyer receptiveness to generic, high-volume outreach) and both identify the same organizational obstacle to full realization: sales cultures built around relationship intuition rather than data-driven decision-making, which resist the behavioral and structural changes AI-driven sales transformation actually requires.

<span class="src">4 key insights that show agentic AI is winning in sales</span> (Oliver Wyman, 2026) extends this concept's predictive and generative AI layers with survey evidence on autonomous agents. Agentic tools released by CRM vendors from 2024 can scan for ideal-client matches, enrich and de-duplicate records, score leads for fit and urgency, monitor buying intent and automate CRM updates and meeting summaries, so reps shift toward oversight and validation and more time selling. Impact is moderate to transformational at the top of the funnel, weaker on operating cost, customer satisfaction and cycle length, and weakest in mid-funnel handoffs where agent outputs are directly experienced by buyers and relevance errors are costly. The five recommended steps are time-boxed (90-180 day) upper-funnel pilots, mandatory CRM integration, credible change champions with clear incentives, staged autonomy as trust and data quality improve, and governance scaled in parallel with a disciplined KPI and ROI review cadence. <span class="src">4 key insights that show agentic AI is winning in sales</span>

<span class="src">The future of B2B sales How growth champions rewire their playbooks with AI</span> (McKinsey, July 2026) argues value comes from rewiring end-to-end workflows rather than rolling out enterprise-wide tools, which "often just automates complexity". Agents scan directories, filings, hiring and procurement signals to build warm leads with a recommended play; tailor coverage by customer value (copilots for high-value accounts, agent-led motion for lower tiers, with next-best-experience programmes reporting 5-8% revenue uplift and 20-30% lower cost-to-serve); build a live customer 360 and generate pre-meeting notes and voice-to-CRM capture; act as deal calculators and autonomous pricing agents (65-85% of pricing executives expect to adopt gen or agentic AI within one to three years, up from 10-30%); and run a performance enablement cockpit that lets managers coach from every call instead of a sample. Four shifts make this stick: a business-led roadmap, human-agent talent and operating models (new roles such as AI workflow designers and agent managers, flatter structures), a data and technology spine with business-owned data domains, and adoption designed in from day one. Executives quoted from Equinix, Dechra and Covestro stress that sellers will manage agents while building trust and co-creating solutions. <span class="src">The future of B2B sales How growth champions rewire their playbooks with AI</span>

## Open Questions

- Neither source addresses how AI-driven SPM's account-scoring and territory-optimization outputs should feed into or interact with individual reps' AI-assisted discovery process — whether, for example, a rep should adjust their discovery approach based on an account's predicted score, or the two systems operate independently in practice.
- The discovery article's case examples are self-reported success stories from the authors' own coaching engagements rather than independently verified outcomes, and the SPM article's central case study (NovaMed) is explicitly a composite, fictionalized example rather than a single verifiable real company — both sources' evidentiary strength should be read accordingly.
- Oliver Wyman's sample is 100 leaders at companies already using agentic AI, so it excludes failed or abandoned deployments and likely overstates average impact; McKinsey's journey results are client examples without stated baselines.
