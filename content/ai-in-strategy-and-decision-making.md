## Summary

AI relaxes the cognitive limits that shaped strategy work by generating thousands of options, building living models and staging structured challenge, and decision agents now bring evidence into executive committees. Sources agree that judgment, proprietary data and critical thinking remain the human strategist's differentiators.

## Highlights

- Felipe Csaszar argues strategy frameworks such as SWOT, the growth-share matrix and Porter's five forces are simple because of bounded rationality, and that AI can "unbound" three tasks: searching options (a McKinsey-documented scouting system scored over 500 acquisition targets in under a day and narrowed them to 15 leads), representing environments (MYbank's 3,000-variable credit model has lent to over 53 million small businesses, 72% first-time borrowers, at about a 1% default rate) and aggregating judgment through creator-critic-competitor agent debate. <span class="src">AI Is Revolutionizing Strategic Decision-Making</span>
- Research cited shows LLM-written business plans rated above entrepreneurs' plans by experienced investors, and a Procter & Gamble experiment with 776 professionals found individuals using AI matched two-person teams without it, with AI teams about 12% faster and likelier to produce top-decile ideas. <span class="src">AI Is Revolutionizing Strategic Decision-Making</span>
- Because models are widely available, lasting strategic advantage comes from proprietary data (Morgan Stanley's adviser assistant reached 98% adoption), proprietary processes (John Deere's See & Spray) and speed to the new frontier (Duolingo's Birdbrain), and the strategist's role shifts from analyst to architect of the decision process. <span class="src">AI Is Revolutionizing Strategic Decision-Making</span>
- A 2026 BCG survey of 175 chief strategy officers finds AI commoditising analysis and names four modes of thinking to cultivate — systems, counterfactual, integrative and critical thinking — noting that only 12% feel well prepared to reinvent advantage continuously and only 2% of companies manage the short-term versus long-term trade-off well. <span class="src">The Next-Gen Strategist in the Age of AI</span>
- BCG's decision agents synthesise cross-functional evidence, test scenarios live during executive meetings and make trade-offs transparent, yet fewer than a fifth of leaders rank decision-making among top AI priorities and only 14% of agent investment goes there, even among firms calling it strategic. <span class="src">Decision Agents Bring AI into the Boardroom</span>
- Early (2023) experiments with ChatGPT found it useful for ideas and storytelling but generic, unable to use proprietary inside knowledge or analogy-based counterfactuals, and most valuable to experienced strategists who can sift its output. <span class="src">Three Lessons From Chatting About Strategy With ChatGPT</span>
- Scott Anthony's four behaviours of horizon scanners are curiosity (try emerging technology first-hand), a scientific approach (superforecasting habits), humility (Christensen's misjudged iPhone prediction) and storytelling, with the rule "bet on demographics, but hedge on systems." <span class="src">Four Keys to Effective Horizon Scanning</span>

## Concept

Csaszar's HBR article (September 2026) frames strategy as three cognitive tasks — searching for courses of action, representing the environment, and aggregating people's judgment — each historically capped by limited attention, politics and the clock. AI changes each: option generation costs fall so far that teams can screen an entire universe before choosing, models of markets and competitors can be rebuilt as continuously updated high-resolution representations (Unilever's ice-cream business integrates weather, demand signals and telemetry from roughly 3 million connected freezers), and synthetic deliberation can pressure-test plans without hierarchy or groupthink. The playbook is to widen the option set before narrowing it, replace static snapshots with living models, institutionalise structured AI challenge through creator, critic and competitor agents, and redefine the strategist as a hybrid who frames questions, designs the AI workflow and knows where machines must yield; leaders should require an AI-expanded list of rejected alternatives, a current competitive model and a structured critique before proposals reach the table. The author cautions that LLMs can produce confident, subtly wrong analysis, so human judgment becomes more important, and answers the "everyone has the same AI" objection with an internet-in-1995 analogy: advantage lies in data, processes and speed built on top of commodity models. <span class="src">AI Is Revolutionizing Strategic Decision-Making</span>

BCG's strategy-function research reaches a similar conclusion from the practitioner side. Chief strategy officers named four untamed issues (rising complexity, continuous reinvention, short-term versus long-term trade-offs and using AI itself) and BCG maps a thinking mode to each, with trigger questions and routines. Systems thinking handles interacting variables, backed by cross-unit forums and second-order-effects exercises, and contextual factors such as geopolitics and technology now explain almost half of variation in profitability. Counterfactual thinking asks what would be built from scratch, as Fujifilm did when it audited more than 70 transferable technologies while Kodak optimised within film, supported by pre-mortems and scenario reviews. Integrative thinking treats trade-offs as both/and, through dual-horizon budgeting and joint CFO-CSO resource allocation. Critical thinking about AI output requires assumption audits, a red-team rotation and explicit human-override rules, since the cost of being wrong increasingly outweighs the cost of being slow. <span class="src">The Next-Gen Strategist in the Age of AI</span>

BCG's decision-agent article applies the idea to the executive committee. Execution agents automate tasks, while decision agents operate at the point of choice, establishing a common evidence base from structured and unstructured inputs, running scenarios mid-meeting as assumptions change, and limiting asymmetric preparation and selective inputs. Use cases are supply-chain planning (a digital twin reconciling sales, manufacturing and procurement incentives), product-feature prioritisation and risk intelligence that converts risks into a common contribution-margin measure and a breakeven for mitigation. BCG recommends piloting on one cross-functional, high-friction decision such as integrated business planning, building governance over data ownership and logic, a shared data and semantic layer, and treating the agent as a structural investment that also exposes unclear accountability. <span class="src">Decision Agents Bring AI into the Boardroom</span>

Two older pieces mark the limits. In 2023 Stadler and Reeves put strategy questions to ChatGPT and found interesting input rather than infallible recommendations: it matched ideas from a prior workshop, hedged more in newer versions, and lacked inside knowledge and analogical reasoning, so experienced strategists benefit most while novices are likelier to be misled, and writing quality complements strategists' blind spot (only 28% of managers can name three of their organisation's strategic priorities). Anthony's horizon-scanning guide supplies the human habits the AI-era strategist still needs, stressing first-hand experimentation, forecast updating and storytelling that turns insight into collective action, as in Kennedy's 1961 moon-landing speech. <span class="src">Three Lessons From Chatting About Strategy With ChatGPT</span> <span class="src">Four Keys to Effective Horizon Scanning</span>

## Open Questions

- Csaszar's evidence for AI beating human strategists comes from experiments with plan evaluation and generation, and the article itself notes LLMs can produce "trendslop" or fabricated evidence; the sources give no field data on strategy outcomes.
- BCG's decision-agent claims come from client pilots of its own product without published results, and its survey shows investment in decision agents lagging well behind stated priority.
- The 2023 ChatGPT experiments predate agentic and reasoning models, and the source itself notes substance changed little between versions, leaving open how far the limits cited there still hold.
